# Filling the script templates (Step 3)

`script-templates.md` comes from the Content Researcher's study of winning reels: each template is a winning reel's script with every specific turned into a `[blank]`, in a short version (20 to 45 seconds, the default) and a full one. The Repurposer fills those blanks with the ideas from the long piece. Proven shapes, the owner's topic.

Look for it with the owner's other files (`my-content-team/script-templates.md`, the Project files, or the chat).

## How many pieces

The owner picks. Ask once, in the moments checkpoint: "How many pieces do you want? I planned 10." If they went straight through without a number, make 10. Never pad: a thin piece gets fewer (`finding-moments.md`).

## The mix

| Has `script-templates.md`? | Default for 10 |
|---|---|
| Yes | 6 short video scripts built on templates, each with its captions; 2 posts; 1 carousel; 1 email blurb (only if the owner has email, else 1 more post) |
| No | 4 short video scripts, each with its captions; 4 posts; 1 carousel; 1 email blurb (only if the owner has email, else 1 more post) |

- The owner's words beat the default: "scripts only", "just carousels", "LinkedIn posts" and so on. Make what they asked for.
- Another number: keep about the same balance (roughly 6 in 10 scripts with templates, 4 in 10 without), at least 1 carousel once there are 5 or more pieces, unless the owner said otherwise.
- The swaps in `piece-formats.md` still apply: not on camera means faceless scripts, no video at all means mostly posts.
- Filming capacity still limits which week the scripts go in (`repurposed-file.md`), not how many you write.

## Matching an idea to a template

For each script, pick the idea first, then the template that fits it best. Read each template's tags: Hook type, Format, "Best for", and whether it "needs you on camera".

1. **Fit the idea's kind to "Best for".** A surprising finding or a myth fits a template built on a claim or a reveal. A list of signs fits a list template. A story template ("the person who changed how I work", "how I feel since the big change") fits only a story the owner can tell as theirs.
2. **On camera?** If the profile says the owner doesn't go on camera, skip templates marked "needs you on camera", or turn them faceless (text on screen, voiceover) and say so in the card's notes.
3. **Can the blanks be filled honestly?** Read the template's blanks. If most of them need things the source and the owner's files don't have, it's the wrong template for this idea.
4. **Spread them out.** Use each template at most twice in one set, so the pieces don't all sound the same. Using every template once is good. **Only 1 or 2 templates?** "At most twice" still wins: once each template is used twice, the rest of the scripts use the no-fit line in step 5 (the best shape from `script-shape.md`).
5. **No template fits an idea:** build that script on the best video shape in `script-shape.md` instead, and write `Built on: no template fit, the {shape} shape` in the card.

## Filling the blanks

- **Use the short version** (20 to 45 seconds) by default. The full version only when the owner asks for a long video. A template with no short version (an older file): keep its hook, its 3 strongest middle lines and its call to action, in order, and say so in one line in the card's filming notes, the way the Scriptwriter does. A `learned-rules.md` line about length (for example "a bit shorter") beats the default.
- **Keep the template's lines in order, and its connector words** ("if you want", "but", "which is why", "and that part"). They carry the rhythm that made the reel work. You may drop a line, never add a new one, and never change a connector.
- **Fill each blank with:**
  - the idea's facts from the long piece (topic, claim, number, steps), with the credit from `credit-rules.md` when the piece isn't the owner's;
  - the owner's own facts from `business-profile.md` ("Owner's answers" too) for anything about them: their story, their offer, their clients, their call to action.
- **First person blanks are the owner's only.** A blank like `[your own result]`, `[where you were before]`, `[the person who taught you]` or `[what happened]` in an "I" line takes only the owner's real facts. Never fill it with the author's, host's or researcher's story. No owner fact for it: leave the `[blank]` on the card and list it under "Gaps to fill".
- **A blank you can't fill honestly stays a blank.** Never fill one from general knowledge, from the creator whose reel made the template, or by guessing.
- **A fixed line that makes a claim** ("I think the person who ... is going to win", "this changed everything"): keep it only if it fits what the owner believes in their own files; otherwise drop that line. Either way, say it in one line in the card's filming notes, so the owner hears it before they say it on camera.
- **Swap the template's call to action** for one that follows the call to action rules in `caption-rules.md` and the owner's profile.
- **Read it out loud with the blanks filled.** It must sound like the owner talking, not like the creator the template came from. Swap any leftover phrase that isn't theirs for their own words (`voice-guide.md`), keeping the line's job.

## The card

Write each script in the Scriptwriter's filming card layout (`repurposed-file.md`, "Post and card files"): the four hooks, the Say and [SHOW] table with a `Job:` on each beat, the payoff, one call to action, filming notes, gaps. Under the `Shape:` line add the template line, copied from the template's "From" tag, exactly the way the Scriptwriter writes it:

```
Built on: template {N} (@{handle}, score {N})
```

That's how the owner and the rest of the team see which proven reel each video follows. In the pieces table and in your reply, say it in plain words: "built on template 2".

## Captions for each script

Each script comes with its captions, written the Copywriter's way (`caption-rules.md`): one caption per platform the owner uses, main platform first, each in its own code block. The caption adds something the video doesn't say (a detail, the steps, the why, the full source), it doesn't repeat it. One call to action. When the idea came from someone else or a paper, the caption carries the fuller credit (`credit-rules.md`).
