# The shape of a short video script

Most videos that work follow the same simple shape:

1. **Hook** (0 to 3 seconds): all four hooks lined up (see `four-hooks.md`).
2. **Three beats** (the middle): three short steps, points or moments. Each beat has one job, set by the video's shape (see "Video shapes" below). Cut anything that doesn't do its job.
3. **Payoff**: the answer, the result, the "after". This is what the hook promised. Never skip it or hide it behind "follow for part 2".
4. **One call to action**: one thing to do next. Never two.

## Video shapes: what each beat does

Pick the shape that fits the idea, then give each beat its job. The shape is about the middle of the video. The hook type (`formats.md`) is about how it opens, so a How-to video can open with a Warning hook.

Write the shape's name on the card's `Shape:` line, spelled as below. If nothing fits, write `Other` and use three plain beats, each one moving the viewer closer to the payoff.

Rules for every shape:
- Three beats at most. Two is fine for a very short video. A beat can take 1 to 3 lines.
- Each beat does its job and nothing else. If a line doesn't do a job, cut it.
- Facts come from the owner: their files, the post's brief, the chat, or what they said in the source video or blog. A beat that needs a fact you don't have (a price, a time, a result, a name) gets a `[your number]` style gap. Never make one up.
- The jobs are notes for the owner. Never put a shape name or a job in what gets said or shown.
- "Often opens with" is a hint. The owner's performance brief beats it (for example, skip question hooks if the brief says so).

### Before and after
Good for: grooming, cleaning, trades, hair, gardens, anything you can see change.
Often opens with: Before and after.
- Beat 1: the before, up close. Let the viewer see how bad it was.
- Beat 2: the one step or tool that made the biggest difference. Not every step.
- Beat 3: the after coming into view, filmed from the same spot and angle.
- Payoff: before and after side by side, or the customer's real reaction (only if it happened and was filmed).
- Common mistake: walking through every step, so the result shows up too late.

### How-to
Good for: a tip the viewer can do at home.
Often opens with: How-to or Warning.
- Beat 1: the first step, straight away, with what they need in the same shot. No "today I'll show you".
- Beat 2: the step people get wrong, and what happens if they do.
- Beat 3: the last step, or how to check it's done right.
- Payoff: the job done, plus the sign that means it's time to call the owner instead (only if that's true for this job).
- Common mistake: six steps squeezed into 30 seconds. Film the 3 that matter and save the rest for another video.

### Customer question
Good for: anyone. The questions customers really ask the owner.
Often opens with: Question, or a Warning or Surprising fact built from the answer.
- Beat 1: the question, in the words customers actually use (from the owner, the brief or the profile).
- Beat 2: the short, straight answer. Give it here, not at the end.
- Beat 3: the reason, or the one detail that makes the answer make sense. Show it if you can.
- Payoff: what the viewer can do with the answer today: a rule of thumb, a sign to look for, or when to book.
- Common mistake: holding the answer back until the end, or "it depends" with no answer at all.

### Myth
Good for: experts, trades, coaches. Something customers believe that isn't true.
Often opens with: Surprising fact or Warning.
- Beat 1: the myth, said the way people say it.
- Beat 2: what's really true, in one plain sentence.
- Beat 3: proof the viewer can see: show it, test it, or point to what the owner sees on the job.
- Payoff: what to do instead.
- Common mistake: knocking another business or another pro. Go after the belief, never a person or a shop. Proof must be real: no made-up studies or numbers.

### Story
Good for: trust and personality. A real moment from the job.
Often opens with: Story start.
- Beat 1: drop into the moment: who, where, what went wrong. One line of setup, no backstory.
- Beat 2: the turn: what the owner tried, noticed or decided.
- Beat 3: how it ended.
- Payoff: the lesson, in one line about the viewer, not the owner.
- Common mistake: a story the owner never told. Only real stories, with the details the owner gave. Use a customer's or pet's name only if the owner did.

### Behind the scenes
Good for: local businesses and makers. What customers never get to see.
Often opens with: Story start or Surprising fact.
- Beat 1: where we are and what's about to happen. A time on screen helps.
- Beat 2: the part customers never see: the prep, the tools, the care.
- Beat 3: the moment it all comes together.
- Payoff: what the customer gets because it's done this way.
- Common mistake: a pretty montage with no point. Every clip should show something the customer gets.

### Offer
Good for: selling. At most 1 post in 4 (what counts as an offer post: `files-layout.md`).
Often opens with: Update, or a Warning about the problem the offer fixes.
- Beat 1: who it's for and the problem it fixes, in the customer's words.
- Beat 2: what they get, shown, not just listed.
- Beat 3: the real details (price, dates, spots), only as the owner gave them. Missing ones become `[your price]` style gaps.
- Payoff: why now. Only a real reason: a real deadline, real spots left, the season. If there's none, say how easy it is to book.
- Common mistake: a deadline or "only 2 spots left" that isn't true.

### Signs to watch for
Good for: trades, pets, homes, cars, health. "Is this a problem?"
Often opens with: List or Warning.
- Beats 1 to 3: one sign each, the easiest to spot first, each one shown on screen.
- Payoff: what to do if you spot one: the fix they can do, or when to call the owner.
- Common mistake: "3 signs" on screen when the video only has 2 real ones. The number must match, and every sign comes from the owner; a sign you'd add from general knowledge is a `[your sign]` gap or marked `[check this is right for you]`.

## Call to action rules

- Comment a word to get something ("Comment NAILS and I'll send the guide"): **only if the owner has a tool that sends a DM automatically** (check `business-profile.md`). Without one, nobody gets the DM and people feel ignored.
- No DM tool: "link in bio", "call us", "follow for more", "save this for later".
- Bookings goal: point to the booking route in `business-profile.md` (link, phone, DM).

## Length

- Most short videos: 20 to 45 seconds. A talking script of 60 to 110 words.
- Under 20 seconds suits one quick tip or a before and after.
- Up to 90 seconds only if every beat earns its place.
- Read the script out loud at normal speed. Count the seconds.

## Writing for the mouth, not the page

- Short lines. One breath each.
- Contractions: "you're", "don't", "it's".
- The owner's own phrases from `voice-guide.md` (Spoken voice).
- No words the owner would trip over.

## Showing, not just saying

Every line gets a **[SHOW]** note: what's on screen while it's said. Real things: hands working, the before, the after, a close-up, a text pop-up. This is how faceless videos work and how talking videos stay interesting.

## Filming card layout (scripts/{date}-{short-name}.md)

The core of every filming card. The Scriptwriter's `card-layouts.md` adds a few more lines; the ones below are the minimum any teammate writes.

```markdown
# {Short title}
Short name: {from the post's brief in content-plan.md}
From the plan: {P number, or "not in the plan"}
Format: {a word from formats.md}
Hook type: {a word from formats.md}
Shape: {a shape from "Video shapes" above, or Other}
Platform: {Instagram Reels | TikTok | YouTube Shorts | Facebook Reels}
Length: about {N} seconds
Plan tag: {REPEAT | REMIX | TEST}

## The four hooks
- First frame: {what we see}
- First sound: {what we hear}
- Text on screen: "{words}"
- Spoken: "{first sentence}"

## Script
| Part | Say | [SHOW] |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | "{line}" | {what's on screen} |
| Beat 1. Job: {the beat's job from the shape} | "{line}" | {what's on screen} |
| Beat 2. Job: {job} | "{line}" | {what's on screen} |
| Beat 3. Job: {job} | "{line}" | {what's on screen} |
| Payoff | "{line}" | {what's on screen} |
| Call to action | "{line}" | {what's on screen} |

## Payoff
{the line that delivers what the hook promised}

## Call to action
"{one line}"

## Filming notes
- Shots to grab: {list}
- Props: {list or "none"}
- Can be filmed with: {other scripts from the same shoot, if any}
```

The `Job:` note goes in the Part cell of each beat's first row, written for this video ("Job: the straight answer: every 8 weeks"), not copied from the shape word for word. If a beat takes more than one row, the next rows say `Beat 1, continued`. A short video with two beats has two beat rows.
