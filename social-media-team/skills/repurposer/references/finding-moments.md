# Finding the best moments and ideas

A long piece is mostly warm-up, repeats and "so anyway". The good stuff is a handful of moments: tips, claims, stories, numbers, quotes. Find those first. Everything the Repurposer writes is built from them. This works the same for the owner's own video, someone else's podcast, a blog post or a research paper; only the credit changes (`credit-rules.md`).

## Read it twice

1. First read: just read it start to end. Don't take notes yet.
2. Second read: mark every line that would make someone stop scrolling. Use the kinds below.

## Kinds of moment

| Kind | What it looks like | Best turned into |
|---|---|---|
| **Tip** | A clear "do this" the viewer can use today | Short video script, carousel |
| **Steps** | "First... then... then..." | Carousel, short video script |
| **Myth** | "Most people think X, but..." | Short video script, post |
| **Story** | A real moment with a person, a place, a problem | Post, short video script |
| **Quote** | One line that's sharp, funny or true on its own | Quote post, text on screen, email subject |
| **Question answered** | "People always ask me..." and the honest answer | Post, short video script |
| **Warning** | A mistake the customer is making right now | Short video script, post |
| **Fact or number** | A number said or written in the piece (keep it exactly) | Text on screen, carousel slide |
| **Finding** | A research result: what was found, in whom, with its number (keep it exactly) | Short video script, carousel, text post |
| **Claim** | An argument the speaker or writer makes, that the owner could agree with, build on or push back on | Post, short video script |
| **Opinion** | Something the owner believes that others in the trade might not | Post, email |

## What makes a moment strong

- It makes sense on its own, without the rest of the piece.
- It's about the viewer's problem, not about the owner (or the author).
- It fits the owner's customers. A great idea from someone else's piece that has nothing to do with the owner's business is skipped.
- It has something concrete in it: a thing you can see, a number the owner gave, a real example.
- The owner said it with energy. Repeated phrases and lines they clearly love are gold.

Skip: greetings, "thanks for watching", sponsor bits, long tangents, anything the speaker says they're unsure about, anything about other people's private details, and (from someone else's piece) personal stories that only work told by them (`credit-rules.md`). From a paper, skip the literature review (what other studies said) unless the paper's own result needs it, and skip numbers you can't explain in one plain line.

## Write down where it came from

The owner needs to find each moment again. Use the best marker the piece has:
- a timestamp, if the transcript has them: `at 04:12`
- a heading, if the blog post or newsletter has them: `under "Winter prep"`
- a page and section, for a PDF or paper: `p. 9, Results`, or the table: `p. 11, Table 3`
- otherwise the paragraph number: `paragraph 6`
- for voice notes or rough notes with no markers: `near the start`, `middle`, `near the end`, or the bullet number

## Quote the real words

Copy the source's words exactly, up to about 40 words (this list is for the owner to check, not for posting; what goes in a post from someone else's piece follows `credit-rules.md`). You may:
- cut filler (um, like, you know, sort of) and false starts
- cut a sentence short with "..."

You may not reword it, tidy up the grammar or add anything. If a transcript word looks misheard (auto transcripts get trade words and names wrong), keep it and mark it: `[check: "pee trap"?]`. Never guess the right word.

## Credit, when it isn't the owner's

From someone else's piece or a paper, every moment carries its credit after the "where": `(finding, p. 9, Results; credit: Lee and colleagues, 2023)` or `(claim, at 12:40; credit: [host], [show])`. For a paper, the moment holds the number together with who was measured, so nobody uses it without its group.

## How many

- Most pieces: 8 to 15 moments.
- A 3 minute video or a 400 word post might only have 4 to 6. That's fine. Say so.
- A research paper usually has 4 to 8 findings worth posting, plus the paper's own big point. Don't stretch one number into five pieces.
- Fewer than about 4 real moments means the piece is too thin for 10 good pieces. Tell the owner plainly, offer what it can honestly make (for example 5), and ask if they have another piece to add.

## The moments list (what the owner sees)

```markdown
## Best moments in "{title or short description of the piece}"

1. **{short name, 2 to 5 words}** ({kind}, {where})
   "{exact words}"
   Good for: {short video | post | carousel | email | quote}
2. ...

## My plan for your {N}
| # | Piece | Platform | Moment | Built on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Short video script | {platform} | 3 | template 2 |
| 2 | Text post | {platform} | 5 | |
| ... | | | | |
```

"Built on" names the script template for each script (`filling-templates.md`), or the shape when no template fits ("Myth shape"). Leave it blank for posts, or leave the whole column out when there's no `script-templates.md`.

Put a star (★) after the name of your 3 strongest moments, so the owner can see your top picks at a glance.

Then the checkpoint, worded for this step:

Just above it, ask the one question the next step needs: "How many pieces do you want? I planned {N}." (skip it if the owner already said a number).
