# Writing from the study (templates, playbook, breakdowns)

When the Content Researcher has studied winning reels, the owner's files hold what won and how. Use it to shape captions, text posts and carousels. Never wait for it: if none of these files exist, write as usual and don't mention them.

Examples here are made up (a bike repair shop called Spoke & Wheel, and a studied account called @made_up_account) so they never leak into a real owner's posts.

## The files and what you take from each

| File (in a Project: the same name with hyphens) | What you take | What you never take |
|---|---|---|
| `script-templates.md` | Which template a video was built on, its "Best for" line, its "From" tag (which breakdown it came from), its lines as a shape for text posts and carousels, its "Text on screen at the start" line for visual hooks | The creator's handle in anything meant for posting |
| `study/playbook.md` | "Calls to action" (how winners ask, counted), "Captions" (how winning captions are built, counted, when the playbook has that section), "Hooks" (text on screen at the start, counted), "In one line" | Any number, name or story about the creator, in a post |
| `study/{handle}/breakdowns/{code}.md` | Section 0 "Caption" (how the winning caption is built), section 1 (text on screen, first frame), section 6 (the ask: said, on screen, in the caption, when it lands), section 8 "Steal this" | Their sentences, names, brands, events, places, numbers, prices, dates, stories, catchphrases |

Read only `status: done` breakdowns. You don't need the pictures in `frames/`: section 1 already says what's in the first frame.

The owner's own rules always beat the study: `learned-rules.md`, then `voice-guide.md`, then `performance-brief.md` (the owner's own results), then the study. If the winners write long captions and the owner said "shorter", shorter wins.

## 1. The caption shape that won

Do this for every caption pack when `study/playbook.md` or breakdowns exist.

1. **Pick the winners to learn from.** A video built on a template: that template's breakdown (its "From" tag names it). Otherwise: the 2 or 3 highest scoring breakdowns whose "Best for" or topic is closest to this post.
2. **Read how their captions are built**, as moves: how long (one line, a few short lines, a few paragraphs), what the first line does (names a feeling, repeats the hook, a personal line), where the ask sits (last line, middle), what comes with the ask (what they get, why it's easy), how it signs off.
3. **Read how they ask.** The playbook's "Calls to action" section (and its "Captions" section, if it has one), with its counts (for example "comment a word to get a link: 3 of 5 winners"), and each breakdown's section 6.
4. **Write the owner's caption in that shape,** in the owner's voice, with the owner's facts only. Note the shape in the post file's Notes in one line, with the count copied from the playbook when it has one: "Caption shape from the study: a personal first line, what they get, then the ask (3 of 5 winners end on the ask)". Never make up a count.
5. **The ask follows the team's call to action rules, never the study's.** Keep the winners' move (where the ask sits, what comes with it), and swap the route when the owner can't use it:

| The winners... | The owner has a comment-to-DM tool on this platform | The owner doesn't |
|---|---|---|
| ask people to comment a word to get something | "Comment {WORD} and I'll send you {thing}", with a real thing the owner named | The owner's booking route in the same spot, with the same "what you get" line ("the link in my bio is where to start"). Say why in one line: "Your winners ask people to comment a word. You don't have a comment-to-DM tool, so I used your link in bio." |
| ask for questions in the comments | A question at the end can be the one call to action, if it fits the goal | The same |
| ask nothing | Still one call to action (team rule), kept short and soft at the very end | The same |
| say the ask out loud and in the caption | The caption's ask matches the video's spoken ask, when that ask follows the rules | The same |

6. **Nothing carries over from their caption** except the shape: no "it's free", no dates, no prices, no "link to register", unless the owner's facts say it.

## 2. A video built on a template

- **How you know:** the filming card in `scripts/` has a `Built on:` line (the Scriptwriter adds it). No card, but the owner says "I used template 2": use that. Otherwise the video wasn't built on a template: don't name one.
- **Name it, the way the Scriptwriter does.** Copy the card's line exactly into the post file header: `Built on: template {N} (@{handle}, score {N})`. In the reply, one line: "Built on template {N}, same as your script." Never put the handle or the score in a caption.
- **Match the template.** The caption does the same job as the template's "Best for" line and points to the same kind of ending the template has (a free next step, a question for the comments, a story's lesson), using the owner's real version of it. Its shape comes from that template's breakdown (section 1 above).
- The caption still adds something the video doesn't say. Never change the template, the card or the spoken line.

## 3. Text posts and carousels shaped like a template

A template can give a text post or a carousel its shape. Use one when the owner asks ("use template 3 for a post"), when the plan's brief names one, or when a template's "Best for" fits the idea clearly. Say which in one line and add the `Built on:` line to the post file header (copied from the template's "From" tag).

- **Shape, not words.** Keep the order of the lines and what each one does. Write every line fresh in the owner's words. Short joining words ("but", "so", "here's what", "and that's why") are fine; a run of 5 or more words in a row from a template line is not.
- **Use the short version** if the template has one. No short version (an older file): keep its first line, its 3 strongest middle lines and its ending, the same way the Scriptwriter does. Template lines about the screen ("Text on screen at the start") become the first line of a text post, or the cover of a carousel. Spoken filler that reads badly in text ("Okay,") goes.
- **Text post:** the template's lines, in order, become short paragraphs. The ending becomes the one call to action, routed by the rules.
- **Carousel:** the template's first line is one of the 3 cover options; its counted run ("first", "the second", "lastly") becomes one slide per point; its ending is the call to action slide. All the carousel rules still apply (5 to 7 slides, 15 words a slide at most, counted).
- Every other rule still applies: one call to action, the offer count, honesty, blanks.

Made-up example. A template's short version:
```
If you've been [doing a common thing], stop.
That's why [the problem it causes].
Do this instead: [your fix, in 2 or 3 short steps].
[your call to action]
```
Spoke & Wheel's text post shaped like it, fresh words, the owner's facts:
```
Still pumping your tyres once a month? That's too long.

Tyres lose air every day, even parked. Soft tyres pinch and go flat.

Give them a squeeze every Sunday. Soft? Pump them to the number on the side.

Still going flat after that? Text 555-0100 and bring it in.
```

## 4. Blanks

Fill a template's `[blank]` only with the owner's facts: `business-profile.md` (with "Owner's answers"), the post's brief, the script, this chat. A blank you can't fill stays a `[blank]`, is listed under "Blanks to fill", and its section goes under `## Draft, needs your answer`. Never fill a blank from the creator's reel, caption or numbers, or from general knowledge.

## 5. The leak check (before saving anything built from the study)

Check every caption, slide, text hook and first-frame idea against the breakdowns you read (their caption, spoken lines and words on screen) and the template's lines:

- [ ] No creator's handle, name, brand, product, event, place, number, price, date, story or catchphrase. The handle may appear only in the post file's `Built on:` line and Notes, copied from the card or template.
- [ ] No run of 5 or more of a creator's words in the same order (outside a `[blank]`). Reword it.
- [ ] Every blank filled with the owner's facts, or left as a `[blank]` and listed.
- [ ] The call to action follows the owner's route (section 1, step 5), not the winners'.
