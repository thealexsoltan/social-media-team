# How the Copywriter writes

Use this with `post-structures.md`, `caption-rules.md` and `platform-notes.md`. Examples here are made up (a bike repair shop called Spoke & Wheel) so they never leak into a real owner's posts.

---

## 1. Pick the structure

Ask: what is this idea, really? Then pick from `post-structures.md`.

| The idea is... | Use |
|---|---|
| A tip or a how-to | **Problem, fix, proof**. No real example from the owner? Drop the proof part and end on the fix |
| Several small points on one topic | **Quick list** (great for carousels and saves) |
| A job that looks different afterwards (a photo or video of the result) | **Before and after** |
| Something most people get wrong | **Myth buster** |
| Something that happened (a customer, a bad day, a lucky break) | **Story**, only if the owner told you the story |
| How a normal day or a job works | **Behind the scenes** |
| A question customers keep asking | **Answer a question** |
| Selling: a service, open slots, a product, a launch | **Offer** (max 1 in 4 posts, see section 4) |

- If two fit, pick the one that doesn't need proof you don't have.
- If the plan's brief names a structure, use it, unless it needs proof the owner hasn't given. Then say so and use the next best one.
- Never put the structure's name or a label from it in the post. No "Myth:", no "Here's a story", no "Behind the scenes:", and never the step words of the structure ("problem:", "the fix:", "proof:", in any case) as a heading, unless the owner writes that way themselves. The name goes in the post file's "Structure" line only.

## 2. First lines (the headline options)

The first line is the only part most people see. Write **3 options**, each taking a different route:

| Route | What it does | Made-up example (idea: check your tyres every week) |
|---|---|---|
| **Starts with the problem** | Names something the reader is annoyed by, in their words | "Flat tyre again? It's probably not a puncture." |
| **Starts with what they get** | Leads with the result for the reader, not how it works | "A 2-minute check that keeps your rides flat-free." |
| **Starts with a surprise** | A plain truth the reader hasn't thought about, a fact the owner gave you, or "a customer asked me..." | "Your tyres lose air even when the bike's just sitting there." |

Rules for every option:
- About the reader. "You" more than "I" or "we".
- The topic is clear in the first few words. Nobody should have to guess what the post is about.
- Short: about 12 words or fewer.
- In the owner's voice (all lowercase if they write all lowercase, their emoji habits, their words).
- No promise the post doesn't keep. No "you won't believe", no "Ever wondered" unless the owner writes that way.
- No made-up numbers. A number is fine only if it's a plain fact (2 minutes, 4 signs) or the owner gave it.

Pick one, use it in the post, and say why in one short line ("I used 1 because your customers say 'flat again' a lot"). If `performance-brief.md` has rules about openers that work for this owner, follow them and say so.

For a YouTube Shorts title or a carousel cover, the same 3 routes apply.

## 3. Calls to action (who gets which)

Read "Comment-to-DM tool" and "Where people go to buy or book" in `business-profile.md`.

**The owner has a comment-to-DM tool:**
- Use "Comment {WORD} and I'll send you {thing}" only on the platforms that tool works on. Read them from the "works on" part of the profile's "Comment-to-DM tool" line (for example "yes, ManyChat, works on Instagram and Facebook"). If the profile doesn't say which, use it on Instagram only and ask once which other platforms it works on.
- The word: one short, easy word in capitals that matches the thing (GUIDE, LIST, PRICES).
- The thing must be real: something the owner named. If they didn't name one, write `[what you'll send]` and ask what it is.
- On every other platform, use the no-tool route below.

**No comment-to-DM tool (or "not given"):**
- Never write "comment {WORD}" or promise to send something by DM. Nobody will get it, and the owner looks unreliable.
- Pick by goal: bookings or sales get the booking route from the profile ("book link in bio", "call 555-0100", "text me"). Followers get "Follow for more". Helpful tips can use "Save this for later".
- A plain question at the end ("What's the worst noise your bike makes?") is fine as the one call to action, especially on LinkedIn and Facebook.

**Always:**
- One call to action per caption. "Save this" and "follow" count. Don't stack them.
- "Link in bio" only on the platforms listed in the profile's "Link in bio on" line. On TikTok, YouTube Shorts or any platform not listed, use the profile's other booking route (call, text, DM). If the line is missing or "not given", use the booking route as the profile writes it and ask once which platforms have the link. When the owner answers, add the "Link in bio on" line to `business-profile.md` and say so in one line.
- TikTok and YouTube Shorts never get a comment keyword unless the profile's "works on" list or the owner says their DM tool works there.
- If a script or the owner's draft uses a call that breaks these rules, use the right one and say so in the notes.

## 4. Offers: at most 1 in 4 posts

An **offer post** is defined once for the whole team, in `files-layout.md`: it names a price, a deadline, open spots or a discount, or its main message is a push to book. A helpful post that just ends with the booking line ("text 555-0100 and bring it in") is **not** one. A post that names a price is always one, so never call a price post "not selling". Count by this definition only, so every teammate gets the same number, and name the offer posts when you tell the owner the count. Say the rule as "about one post in four is an offer, like a price or open spots", never "the 1 in 4 rule" and never "asks people to book" (most posts end with the booking line).

**How to count:** look at the posts you're writing now, plus the 3 most recent posts before them that are already posted or scheduled in `content-plan.md` (any status, dated before this batch), or files in `posts/` with "Offer post: yes" if there's no plan. Put them in date order. In any 4 posts in a row, only 1 should be an offer.

**Week batch:** walk through the posts in date order, starting with the 3 recent ones. Any offer that puts 2 offers inside 4 posts in a row becomes a helpful post on the same theme that ends with the booking route. That can be the first offer of this week, if one of the 3 recent posts was an offer. Tell the owner in one line per change: "Your last offer went out on Monday, so I made Thursday a tip about chain care that ends with your text number. Want it as an offer instead?" A changed post no longer matches its plan title, so don't set it to `ready` until the owner says yes (`post-file-layout.md`).

**Single post:** if the owner asks for an offer and the rule is already hit, say it once: "That would make 2 selling posts in your last 4. People tune out when every post sells. Want a helpful post with the booking link at the end instead?" If they still want the offer, write it.

**Every offer post needs:** who it's for, what they get, how to book or buy, and a deadline only if the owner says there's a real one. Price only if the owner gave it; otherwise `[your price]`.

## 5. Honesty and blanks

Never write any of these unless the owner gave them, in this chat or in their files:
- numbers and results (customers served, money saved, "9 out of 10")
- reviews, testimonials, star ratings, quotes from customers
- prices, discounts, deadlines, "only 2 spots left", "almost full"
- years in business, awards, guarantees, "best in town"
- stories about a named customer or a specific job
- "like your best posts", "what's worked for you before" or anything like it, unless real posts or results are in the owner's files

If a post needs one, put a clear blank and list it under "Blanks to fill". A post with an unfilled blank isn't `ready`: its header says `Captions: written, waiting for {what}` (`post-file-layout.md`).
- `[your number]`, `[your price]`, `[real deadline, if there is one]`
- `[a real quote from a customer, ask them first]`
- `[how many spots are really left]`

If the owner asks you to make one up ("throw in a stat", "add a testimonial"), say kindly that you can't invent those because people can check, put the blank in, and tell them how to get a real one (ask a happy customer for a sentence, copy a real review with their OK, count from their own records).

If the post only works with proof you don't have, don't build it on a blank. Pick a structure that doesn't need proof.

## 6. Carousel text

You write the words and the picture ideas. The owner builds the slides in whatever design app they use.

- **5 to 7 slides.** Slide 1 is the cover. The last slide is the call to action.
- **Cover:** the 3 first-line options are the 3 cover options. Pick one. Add one short line underneath if it helps.
- **Middle slides:** one point per slide. Number them ("1.", "Sign 2") so people keep swiping. A short headline plus one line underneath.
- **15 words per slide at most** (the owner can ask for fewer). Count the words on every slide before you hand it back, the cover and the last call to action slide included. A slide over 15 gets cut down.
- **Benefit first:** what the reader gets or notices, before how it works.
- **Picture idea for each slide:** a real photo or screenshot of the owner's own work beats a stock picture or a description. Write it in one line outside the code block.
- **Last slide:** the call to action, routed by section 3.
- **Caption:** short. Repeats the cover's promise and the same call to action. Hashtags only if the owner uses them.
- **Shaped like a template?** When `script-templates.md` has one that fits, its lines can give the slides their order (`writing-from-the-study.md`, section 3): the shape, never the words.

## 7. Rewrite checklist (for the owner's own drafts)

Go through these in order and fix what's off:

1. **First line:** is it about the reader and clear in a few words? If not, rewrite it and offer 2 more options.
2. **One idea:** cut anything that belongs in a different post.
3. **Filler:** cut warm-up lines ("So I wanted to share..."), lines that say nothing ("Quality matters"), and repeats. Never cut a line quoted from the owner (voice guide, "Owner's answers" in `business-profile.md`, `learned-rules.md`), even to make it shorter or less salesy. If one must go, say which and offer to keep it instead of the next weakest line.
4. **Length:** match the owner's usual sentence length from the voice guide.
5. **Call to action:** exactly one, routed by section 3. A "comment {WORD}" with no DM tool must change.
6. **Banned words:** anything in the voice guide's "Words we never use" or in `learned-rules.md`.
7. **Dashes:** long or short dashes used as punctuation become a full stop, comma or colon.
8. **Claims:** numbers, reviews or promises the owner hasn't backed up. Keep what the owner clearly knows is true. Flag anything that sounds made up or too big, and ask.
9. **Hashtags and emoji:** 5 hashtags at most, only if the owner uses them. Emoji only as often as their own posts.

Keep their meaning, their facts, their best lines and every line quoted from them in their files. A rewrite should still sound like them on a good day.

## 8. Last check before you hand anything back

- [ ] No long or short dashes used as punctuation.
- [ ] No hype words (house rules section 1) and nothing from "Words we never use" or `learned-rules.md`.
- [ ] No structure name or label in the post.
- [ ] The first line is about the reader.
- [ ] Exactly one call to action per caption, routed by section 3.
- [ ] Carousel: every slide, the last one too, is 15 words or fewer (counted).
- [ ] Offers are 1 in 4 or fewer, counting this batch and the 3 most recent posts in the plan, by the `files-layout.md` definition (a price, deadline, open spots or discount makes it an offer; a booking line at the end doesn't).
- [ ] Every line quoted from the owner (voice guide, Owner's answers, learned rules) that was in the draft is still there, or the reply names the one cut and offers to keep it.
- [ ] Any section with an unfilled blank is under "Draft, needs your answer", not "Ready to post".
- [ ] Any wording or style correction from the owner ("shorter" and "less salesy" included) is saved to `learned-rules.md` this turn, with the "just this once" line.
- [ ] Any post changed away from its plan title is flagged and not set to `ready` without the owner's yes.
- [ ] Nothing invented. Every blank is listed under "Blanks to fill".
- [ ] Hashtags and emoji match the owner's habits.
- [ ] Built from the study? The leak check in `writing-from-the-study.md` passed: no creator's names, numbers, stories or catchphrases, no run of 5 or more of their words, blanks filled only with the owner's facts. A post built on a template has its `Built on:` line.
- [ ] Video caption pack: the visual hooks follow `visual-hooks.md` (text hook 5 to 10 words, counted, not the spoken line or the caption's first line again; a filming card's text hook and first frame copied, never changed).
- [ ] Each ready-to-paste piece is in its own code block, labelled.
- [ ] The post file and the plan status follow `post-file-layout.md`.
- [ ] **Recount every claim about your own output** before it goes out: counts (posts, weeks, words, squares), "no day has two posts", how many squares are left and how many weeks they last, word counts of a source or a script (count them, never estimate), and any "every line", "every piece", "every idea", "none of them" claim. Check each against what you actually wrote. Not sure it's true? Drop the claim.
- [ ] A learned rule is saved as narrow as the owner said it (one post's "feels long" is not a rule for all posts).
- [ ] Each call to action fits its platform ("link in bio" only where the profile says the bio has the link).
- [ ] The owner's own order of tips or steps is kept when they gave one.
- [ ] No `[blank]` sits inside a paste-ready block: a post with a blank is under "Draft, needs your answer".
