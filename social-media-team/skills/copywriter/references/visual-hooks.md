# Visual hooks (what people see in the first 2 seconds)

Two of the four hooks (`four-hooks.md`) work with the sound off: the **text hook** (the words on screen at the start) and the **first frame** (the very first picture). Many people scroll with the sound off, so these two often decide if anyone stays. The Copywriter writes them in every caption pack for a video, and on their own in Visual hooks mode.

The other two hooks (the spoken line and the first sound) belong to the Scriptwriter. A new spoken line, all four hooks, or a script: "The Scriptwriter does that. Type /scriptwriter."

Examples here are made up (Spoke & Wheel, a bike repair shop) so they never leak into a real owner's posts.

## What you hand back

One visual hook in use and 2 alternatives. Each one has:

```
Text on screen: "{5 to 10 words, 1 or 2 short lines}"
First frame: {one line: what we see in the very first picture}
From: {your filming card | template {N} | your winner {post, score} | a winner you studied ({account}, score {N}) | fresh, not proven yet}
Why it might work: {one plain line tied to where it came from}
```

The alternatives each take a different angle from the one in use: what's at stake, the result, the surprise, who it's for, how quick it is.

**Never give away the payoff in the first frame.** If the video's payoff is the result (the finished groom, the fixed leak, the answer), an alternative's first frame and text on screen tease it, show the problem or the start, but never show or say the result itself, or nobody needs to keep watching. A before and after video may open on the before, never on the after.

## Where the one in use comes from

1. **A filming card exists** (`scripts/`, matched by short name): its "Text on screen" and "First frame" lines are the ones in use. Copy them exactly, marked `From: your filming card`. Never change them, never contradict them, and never edit the card (it's the Scriptwriter's file). Your 2 alternatives must fit the same video: the same spoken line, the same topic and promise, filmable in the same place with the same props. They are offers only.
   - If the card's text hook breaks a rule below (it repeats the spoken line, it's over 10 words, it uses a never-say word), say so in one line and make your fix the first alternative. Still don't edit the card.
   - If the owner picks an alternative, write "In use: alternative {A or B}, your pick ({date})" in the post file's Visual hooks section, and say: "Use it when you film. The Scriptwriter can update the card: type /scriptwriter." (Run by the Orchestrator: name the Scriptwriter, no command.)
2. **No card**, but a script, a transcript or a filmed video the owner described: you write the one in use, built from the best source below.
3. **Nothing about the video yet:** ask for the spoken line or what happens in the video first. Never guess.

## Sources for new ones (best first)

Tie each one to where it came from. Use the first sources that give you something; fill the rest with fresh ones.

1. **The template the video was built on** (`script-templates.md`): its "Text on screen at the start" line, filled with the owner's facts. If it has none, its first line's move.
2. **The study** (`study/playbook.md` "Hooks", each breakdown's section 1, `research-notes.md` "Top hooks"): the move behind a winner's text on screen or first frame (a question people ask you, a before and after, your result next to a feeling). Model the move, never the words: no run of 5 or more of their words in a row, none of their numbers, names or stories.
3. **The owner's own winners** (`performance-brief.md`, "Your winning first lines"): the pattern, with new words.
4. **Fresh, not proven yet:** from the four hooks' kinds (warning, surprising fact, before and after, question, story start, list).

Never claim a hook will get views. A score in the "Why" line is copied from the owner's files, never made up.

## Rules for the text hook

- **5 to 10 words**, 1 or 2 short lines. Count them.
- **Adds to the spoken line, never repeats it.** It gives what the spoken line doesn't: who it's for, what's at stake, the result, the surprise. Not the spoken line again, not even shortened.
- **Works with the sound off:** someone who only reads it knows what the video is about.
- **Not the caption's first line again** either: on Instagram both show at the same time.
- Plain words a 15 year old gets. The owner's habits (all lowercase if they write that way, their emoji, or none).
- No hype words, nothing from the owner's never-say list or `learned-rules.md`, no number the owner didn't give.
- Only promise what the video delivers.

Made-up example. Spoken line: "Flat tyre again? It's probably not a puncture."
- Good text hook: "the 2 minute check that stops it" (adds the fix and how quick it is)
- Bad text hook: "flat tyre again? probably not a puncture" (the spoken line again)

## Rules for the first frame

- One line: what's on screen in the very first picture, with something moving or changing (hands at work, a before, something pushed toward the camera). A still talking head is the weakest first frame: if the owner talks to camera, give them something to do in the first second.
- Only things the owner has, does or showed (their place, their props, from the profile, the script or this chat). Nothing from the owner's "Never say or show".
- The text hook, the first frame and the spoken line all point the same way: the same topic, in the first 2 seconds.

## In the post file

A caption pack for a video gets a `## Visual hooks` section, right after "First line options" (`post-file-layout.md`). In the reply, show the one in use and the 2 alternatives in a short block, after the captions.
