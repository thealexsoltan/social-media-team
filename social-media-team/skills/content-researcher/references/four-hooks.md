# The four hooks

The first 2 seconds decide if anyone watches. In those 2 seconds a viewer gets four things at once. All four have to point the same way.

| Hook | What it is | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Spoken hook** | The first sentence said out loud | Says what the video is about and why to stay |
| **Text hook** | The words on screen at the start, 5 to 10 words on 1 or 2 short lines | Adds to the spoken hook (doesn't just repeat it), and works with the sound off |
| **First frame** | What we see in the very first frame | Shows the topic before anyone reads or hears anything |
| **First sound** | The first noise: a voice mid-sentence, a clip, a snap, a splash | Grabs attention in the first split second |

Many short videos have no talking at all. Then the text hook and first frame do all the work. Plan them just as carefully.

## Rules that keep hooks strong

- Make the topic clear within 2 seconds. A clever line nobody understands loses people.
- Talk to "you", not "I". "Your water heater is about to..." beats "I want to talk about water heaters."
- Plain words. If a 15 year old wouldn't get it, change it.
- One idea. Don't promise three things.
- Movement or change in the first frame (something happening, a before and after, hands at work). A still talking head is the weakest first frame.
- Only promise what the video delivers. A hook that tricks people gets swiped and hurts the account.

## Kinds of hook (use them as starting points, not fill-in templates)

- **Warning**: tells you about a mistake you might be making
- **Surprising fact**: something the viewer believes that isn't true
- **Before and after**: shows the change, then explains it
- **Question you'd actually ask**: the question customers really ask the owner
- **Story start**: drops you into the middle of a moment
- **List**: "3 signs that..." (use the real number of points in the video)
- **Challenge**: "Bet you didn't know..." (careful: must pay off)

## Checking why a video flopped

Go through the four hooks in order and ask:
1. First frame: could you tell the topic with the sound off and no text?
2. Text hook: was it readable in under 2 seconds, and did it add something?
3. Spoken hook: was the topic clear in the first sentence?
4. First sound: was there dead air, a slow "hey guys", or a boring start?

The first "no" is the most likely reason. Say "most likely", not "the reason".
