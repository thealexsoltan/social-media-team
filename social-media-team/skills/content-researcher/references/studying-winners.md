# Studying winners and writing the brief

We study winners to find **moves** the owner can reuse with their own customers. We never copy someone's words, footage or jokes.

## Which posts to study

- Only posts with an outlier score of 2.0 or more (see `outlier-score.md`). Best score first.
- Up to 3 winners per account is plenty. 8 to 15 winners across all accounts makes a solid brief.
- Also glance at the 1 or 2 lowest posts of a strong account. If they're the same topic as a winner, that's a "two posts that look alike" pair, and the best lesson you'll get.
- Pinned all-time hits: note them, but don't let them drive the brief. They're often years old.

## When the whole account is winning

Some accounts have no single winners because every post does well (the whole-account check in `outlier-score.md`). Then don't study one post. Study **what their posts have in common**:

1. Take the posts used for the normal (up to 15 is enough).
2. For each, note what you can see: format, topic, how it opens (hook type and first frame), length, call to action. Same rules as the checklist below: `not seen` when you can't see it.
3. Count each one as "x of y": "8 of 10 posts are Hands at work", "7 of 10 open on the dog's face close up", "9 of 10 are under 20 seconds". Only count posts where you could actually see that thing, and say so: "length seen on 6 of 10".
4. Anything in **at least half** the posts is a habit worth borrowing. Anything less is "mixed".
5. Write it in the "Whole account winning" section of `research-notes.md` (layout in `research-files.md`).

These habits count toward patterns like any winner: a habit seen in this account and in a winner from another account is a pattern from 2 accounts.

If the sample is under 9 posts, say "small sample" at the top and note that we'll check the account again once it has more posts.

## The checklist for each winner

Fill every line. If you can't see it, write `not seen`. If the owner told you there's none, write `none` or `no talking`.

| Line | Where it comes from | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Score and sum | grid screenshot or Apify | "26,300 ÷ 3,200 = 8.2" |
| Topic | caption, cover, words on screen | One plain line, what the viewer learns or sees |
| Format | cover, post screenshot, owner | Only the words in `formats.md` |
| Hook type | the four hooks below, your work | Only the words in `formats.md`. Pick what the first 2 seconds lean on; if the opening wasn't seen, give your best pick from the caption and cover marked `(guessed from caption)`, never `not seen` |
| Length | owner, Apify, or a player screenshot showing the time | Seconds |
| Spoken hook | owner typed it, captions screenshot | The first sentence, word for word |
| Text hook | cover or first frame screenshot | Words on screen at the start, word for word. Words you only see later in the video go here marked "(seen mid-video, not confirmed at the start)" |
| First frame | first frame screenshot, or the cover (say "cover only") | What we see, in one line |
| First sound | owner | Voice mid-sentence, music, a noise (clippers, water, a door) |
| CTA | caption, last words on screen | The exact ask: "book link in bio", "comment PRICE", "call us" |
| Hook pattern | your work | See below |
| Why it may have worked | your work | Always "best guess". Tie it to the four hooks or the topic |
| What to borrow | your work | The move, rewritten for this owner's business |

Never guess what happens in the middle of a video you haven't seen. Study what you were shown.

## Format and hook type

Use only the words in `formats.md`, so the plan, the scripts, the research and the results can all be counted together. Every winner gets one format and one hook type.

- Mixed? Pick the format most of the video looks like. You can add what it's over in brackets: "Text on screen only (over a hallway at night)".
- Nothing fits, for example an acted skit or a customer reacting? Write `Other` and describe it in a few words: "Other (acted skit, two people)".
- "Before and after" is a hook type, not a format (see `formats.md`).

## Hook patterns

A hook pattern is the hook with the specific words swapped for blanks in square brackets, so the owner can reuse the move.

- At most 3 blanks. Fewer blanks keeps it reusable.
- It must still read as a sentence.
- A label like "Warning" or "curiosity" is a kind of hook, not a pattern. Give the hook type too (words in `formats.md`), but always give the pattern.
- No talking and no words on screen? Then the pattern is the first frame: "[problem shown close up], then [hands fixing it]".

Example (made up):
- Hook: "If your doodle looks fine on top, check behind the ears."
- Pattern: "If your [thing] looks fine on top, check [hidden spot]."
- Hook type: Warning.

## Finding patterns

Count, don't guess. A pattern is something that shows up in **at least 2 winners from at least 2 accounts**. Write it as a count: "4 of 9 winners open on the problem close up (@handle 4.6, @handle 3.1, @handle 2.5, @handle 2.2)". A move seen once is a single example, not a pattern. Put it under Top hooks, not under Patterns to borrow.

Count formats and hook types the same way, using the `formats.md` words, to fill "Formats that win" and "Hook types that win".

## Writing the Competitor Brief

The brief is for a busy owner and for the Content Strategist. Keep it short.

1. **In short**: 3 to 5 lines. What their winners have in common, which format wins, which topic wins, and the one move to try first. A "best topic" or "best format" follows the pattern rule above: it needs winners from 2 or more accounts. If it doesn't have them, write "not a pattern yet" instead of naming a winner.
2. **Patterns to borrow**: at most 5, strongest first. Each names the accounts and scores, and says how this owner could use it with their own customers and what they can film.
3. **Hooks to test**: 3 to 6 patterns, each with a version for this owner. Write the version in the owner's voice (read `voice-guide.md` and `learned-rules.md` first). Don't put numbers, prices or results in it that the owner never gave. Use `[your number]` instead. If a hook names a time ("this weekend", "tomorrow"), check it against the event date and today's date.
4. **What not to copy**: moves that don't fit (needs 3 people, needs a face when the owner won't show one, uses words in the owner's "never say" list, makes claims they can't back up).
5. **What we couldn't see**: small samples, missing first sounds, accounts we only saw covers for. This keeps everyone honest.

Fit checks before anything goes in the brief:
- Could this owner film it with what they have? (On camera or not, videos per week, alone or with help.)
- Does it point toward their goal? (Bookings, sales, leads, followers.)
- Is it about their customers, not the competitor's?

If the owner has their own numbers (`results-log.md`, or a screenshot of their own grid), fill the "Your own account" section of `competitors.md` (layout in `competitor-mix.md`) and add one line to the brief: "Your normal is [N]. Theirs range from [N] to [N]." Never compare their full history with someone's best posts only. If the comparison isn't fair, say why. Then run "Your version vs theirs" below and put its strongest finding in "In short".

## Your version vs theirs

The most useful thing we can give a beginner: one of **their own** posts next to a competitor winner **on the same topic**, and one thing to change next time.

**What you need**
- The owner's post: from `results-log.md` (its latest check row, plus its row in "What's in each post"), or from a screenshot of their own grid plus what they tell you about the post.
- A competitor winner from "Top hooks" in `research-notes.md`, studied with the checklist above.
- The same topic means the viewer learns or sees the same thing ("hidden mats under a fluffy coat"), not just the same broad area ("grooming"). If you're not sure two topics match, ask the owner in one line.

**Scores, never raw views.** Each post is scored against **its own account's normal**: the owner's post against the owner's normal, the competitor's against theirs (`outlier-score.md`). "Their 16,300 views against your 900" means nothing on its own, because they have a bigger audience. Show both sums. The owner's post can't get a fair score when:
- it's under 3 days old (`too new`) or was boosted. Say so and leave the score out.
- the owner has fewer than 9 posts old enough to count (`no normal yet`, or a grid with fewer than 9 readable counts). Say "no fair score yet", compare the hooks only, and ask for a screenshot showing more posts (unless the owner declined screenshots).

**Pick the pairs.** Up to 3, the clearest same-topic matches first. If the owner names a pair, use that one. If the owner's post scored as well as or better than the competitor's, say so plainly: that's a move to keep, not a fix.

**Side by side.** Use the layout in `research-files.md` ("Your version vs theirs"). One row each for the four hooks, then format, length and CTA. Write each cell exactly as the files or screenshots have it, short names and scores included. The last column says `differs`, `same` or `can't tell`:
- `can't tell` whenever either side is `not seen`, `not known` or only `guessed from caption`. Only hooks that were seen on both sides can be compared. Never fill a cell from what the post "probably" did.
- If 3 or more of the four hooks are `can't tell`, don't guess a fix. Say what's needed instead ("type the first sentence you said in your video") and stop there for that pair.

**One thing to try.** Pick the clearest difference among the rows marked `differs`, and turn it into one change for the owner's next post on this topic, marked "best guess". Make it something they can film with what they have (on camera or not, alone or with help), in their voice, with no words lifted from the competitor. One change, not a list: if the next post does better, we'll know why.

Fill-in example (made up):

> Your "matted-doodle-shave" scored 0.6 (900 ÷ your normal 1,500). Their winner on the same topic scored 8.2 (26,300 ÷ their normal 3,200). The clearest difference is the first frame: yours opens on you smiling at the camera, theirs opens on the mats close up. Try next time (best guess): open on your hands parting the coat to show the mat, then say your first line.

## Things we never write

- How many sales, bookings, leads or dollars a competitor got from a post. Nobody outside that business knows.
- Why a post "definitely" worked. It's always a best guess.
- Any view count, like count or date you didn't see.
- A competitor's words as the owner's hook. Borrow the move, write new words.
