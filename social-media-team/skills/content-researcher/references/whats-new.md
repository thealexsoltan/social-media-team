# What's new in your niche

Fresh things to post about, found with web search. The point is to be early: a post about something new does better than the same post a month late, when everyone has made one.

## Before you search

1. Read the profile: trade, town or area, best customer, platforms.
2. Check `research-notes.md` ("Where we look for news"). If it's empty, search anyway and add the useful sources you find. Don't ask the owner where news shows up unless you can't search the web (see below).
3. Note today's date. Everything is judged against it.

## The four kinds of thing to look for

| Kind | What it is | How to search | Freshness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **News** | Rule changes, price changes, recalls, new products, warnings, things in the local paper about the trade | "{trade} news", "{trade} {this month} {year}", "{town} {trade}", the owner's sources | Published in the last 30 days |
| **Season** | What the next 4 to 6 weeks bring: weather, holidays, school terms, busy and quiet times for the trade | Think it through from the date and the place. Search only to check a date (a local event, a holiday that moves) | Coming up in the next 6 weeks |
| **Local** | Events, openings, weather warnings, roadworks, anything in the owner's area that touches the trade (only if they serve one area) | "{town} events {month}", "{town} {trade}", local news sites | Coming up or in the last 2 weeks |
| **Question** | What people are asking right now | Search the questions customers might type ("why does my...", "how often should I...", "is it worth..."). Look at forums, "people also ask" boxes and Q&A pages the search shows you | Asked recently, or asked again and again |

## How to judge an item

Keep it only if it passes all of these:
- **It's real.** You saw it on a real page, and you have the link and the date. Never fill a gap from memory. Look for the date on the page (top, byline, bottom) before giving up; only then write `date not shown`. Anything older than a year isn't news: leave it out or say how old it is.
- **It fits this business.** The owner's customers would care, and the owner can talk about it honestly.
- **It can be shown.** The owner can film something for it (hands at work, a before and after, the thing itself). If it can only be talked about, say so.
- **It has a hook.** You can say in one line why someone would stop scrolling.

Leave out: things older than 30 days (unless it's a question people keep asking), anything you listed in the last notes, scary stories the owner can't help with, politics, and other businesses' bad news (don't build posts on someone else's trouble).

Aim for 5 to 8 good items. Fewer real items beat a long list of padding. If you only find 2, say so.

## Writing each item

One row in the "What's new in your niche" table (`research-files.md`):

- **Date**: when it was published, or when it happens.
- **What**: one plain line.
- **Kind**: news, season, local or question.
- **Source**: `[site name](link)`. Season items worked out from the calendar can say `calendar`.
- **Why it's a video**: one line, for this business and its customers.
- **Angle to try**: one hook idea in plain words. The Strategist and Scriptwriter will shape it. Don't add numbers or claims the source doesn't have.
- **Time words**: if the angle says "this weekend", "tomorrow", "next week" or similar, check it against the item's Date and today's date. If they don't match, change the words (or use the day's name).
- **Use by**: the last date the post still makes sense.

After the table, say in 2 or 3 lines which items are the most urgent, and what you looked at but left out (one line, so the owner knows you checked).

## If you can't search the web

Say it plainly: "I can't search the web here, so I can't check the news." Then:
1. Give season items from the calendar only, marked `calendar`.
2. Ask: "What did customers ask you this week? Those make great posts." Skip this if they already gave you customer questions. You may also ask once: "Where does news in your trade show up first? A trade magazine, a supplier, an association, a local Facebook group, a forum?" Ask both in one message.
3. Suggest turning on web search, or pasting a link to a news story you can then read if it's a normal web page (not an Instagram or TikTok link).

Never make up a news story, a date, a statistic or a quote to fill the list.

## Keep it tidy

- Drop rows older than 30 days from "What's new" when you refresh.
- Add useful sources to "Where we look for news" so next week's scan is faster.
- Update "What's new checked" in the "Based on" line at the top of `research-notes.md`.
