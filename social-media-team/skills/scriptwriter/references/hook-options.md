# Hook options: how the Scriptwriter builds them

The owner picks the hook. Our job is to give them a short, varied, honest list to pick from.

## Where hooks come from (in this order)

1. **Your winners.** Read `performance-brief.md` first:
   - **`## Your winning first lines`** comes first. The Data Analyst writes it from the owner's own winners: for each one, the pattern with blanks, the real first line, the post (date and short name) with its score, and how often that opening won. Model your "from your winner" options on these patterns. Use the pattern as the file gives it, and name the post and score exactly as the file does. The real line shows the shape: write new words for this post, don't repeat it word for word.
   - Then its "Best posts to copy from" table, for winners that have no first line listed.
   - No `## Your winning first lines` section, or it only says "Not enough winners yet.": use "Best posts to copy from" and `results-log.md` (posts with a score of 2.0 or more; a `boosted` post doesn't count).
   - Its "Rules to follow" always apply, whichever source you use. When you mention one to the owner, say whether it's strong or an early sign.
2. **Accounts you study.** `research-notes.md`: the "Top hooks (winners we studied)" entries (spoken hook, text hook, first frame, first sound, hook pattern, score) and the "Hooks to test" table.
   - `script-templates.md` (if there is one): each template's first line is a hook pattern from a studied winner, with its account and score in the "From" tag. A template whose format and hook type fit the post comes first among these.
3. **Fresh.** The hook types in `formats.md` (the kinds in `four-hooks.md`), shaped for this post. These are not proven yet, and the label says so.

Name a post the way the file names it (for example `2026-09-26-psa-doodle-mats`, or the account handle and score from the research notes).

Aim for **6 options**: up to 3 from your winners, up to 3 from accounts you study. If the notes hold 2 or more studied patterns, use at least 2 of them, even when their topic differs: borrow the move, not the topic. Fill any gap with fresh ones.

If there are no winners or research notes yet, say so in one line before the list. For example: "You don't have scored winners yet, so these are fresh hooks built on hook types that tend to work. Once the Data Analyst scores your posts, I'll model hooks on your own winners." Never call a hook "proven" unless a file shows it won.

## Model the shape, never copy the words

Take what made the original work: the hook type, how it opens, where the twist lands, what the first frame shows. Then write it fresh for this business, its customer and its words.

- Never lift a competitor's line word for word, or nearly: no run of 5 or more of their words in the same order (outside a `[blank]`). Check every option against the source line before you show it.
- Show the shape as a **pattern with blanks**, so the owner can reuse it on other posts. Example: "If your [thing] does [sign], it's [what it really means]."
- Never mention the other account's name in the hook itself.

## The layout of one option

```
**A. {Hook type}** · from your winner: "{post as named in the file}"
Pattern: "{the shape with [blanks]}"
Spoken: "{the first sentence, for this post}"
Text on screen: "{1 or 2 short lines}"
First frame: {what we see, something moving or changing}
First sound: {what we hear in the first split second}
Why it might work: {one plain line tied to where it came from, see below}
```

`{Hook type}` is one of the words in `formats.md` (Warning, Surprising fact, Before and after, Question, Story start, List, Challenge, How-to, Update, Other).

Labels to use after the hook type:
- `from your winner: "{post}"`
- `from an account you study: "{post}"`
- `from template {N}: "@{handle}, score {N}"` (a template from `script-templates.md`)
- `fresh, not proven yet`

**Never write a view count or a score that isn't in the owner's files.** If the file has no number, leave the number out.

## The "Why it might work" line

One plain line, about 20 words or fewer, on every option (backup hooks too). It gives the owner a reason to pick, tied to the source:

- **From your winner:** name the post and its score as the file shows them, and what this option keeps. Made-up example: `Same shape as your first line on 2026-08-30-porch-steps-before-after (score 2.8): the result shows before anyone talks.`
- **From an account you study, or a template:** the account, its score from `research-notes.md` or the template's "From" tag, and the move you kept. Made-up example: `@example.shop opened on a sound people know from home (score 3.4). This opens on your clippers the same way.`
- **Fresh:** a plain reason from the owner's files or the four-hooks rules. Made-up examples: `Your brief says customers ask this all the time.` or `The first frame shows the problem before anyone reads a word.` Only say customers ask or want something if a file or the chat says so.

Never claim or predict views, likes, follows, bookings or "going viral". No "will get", "proven", "guaranteed" or "always works". A score is a past result and is written as one ("(score 2.6)"), never as a promise.

For a no-talking video, write `Spoken: none (no talking)` and make the text on screen and first frame do the work.

## Variety

- At least 3 different hook types in a set of 6.
- No more than 2 options of the same hook type.
- If a rule in `performance-brief.md` says a hook type doesn't work for this account, leave it out and say so once: "I skipped question hooks, your brief says they haven't worked for you." This holds even when the plan brief's hook direction asks for that kind of hook: the performance brief wins, and you say so in one line.

## Check every option before you show it

- The topic is clear within 2 seconds.
- The spoken hook is short: about 15 words or fewer, under 3 seconds out loud.
- It talks to "you", not "I". (A story start in the owner's own voice is the one exception.)
- Plain words. A 15 year old gets it.
- The text on screen adds something to the spoken line (a detail, the stakes, the result) and makes sense with the sound off. 1 or 2 short lines, about 8 words or fewer.
- The first frame has movement or change. A still talking head is the weakest choice.
- It only promises what the video delivers.
- It follows `learned-rules.md`, the voice guide's "Words we never use" and the profile's "Never say or show".
- **No invented numbers.** A number in a hook must come from the owner's files or chat, or be the real count of points in the video ("3 signs" only if there are 3). Anything else becomes `[your number]`.

## After the list

End with a small question, not a full checkpoint:

> Pick one or more (each pick becomes its own video). Or say "10 more", or "mix A and D", or tweak any of them.

## Follow-ups

- **"10 more" / "these aren't it":** write 10 new ones. Keep the labels and the variety. Don't repeat the first set.
- **"Mix A and D":** take the opening of one and the twist of the other. Show the new hook with all four parts.
- **Text on screen only** (the owner already has a spoken line): give 5 text options, each adding a different thing (what's at risk, how quick it is, proof you've seen it, the surprise, the easy way). Recommend 1 or 2 and say why in a few words.

## Picking a hook yourself

When the owner says "just write it" or skip the options (or in One shoot, three videos, or when the Orchestrator runs you for setup or the weekly loop), pick the strongest option yourself using this order: a winner of yours that fits the topic (start with `## Your winning first lines`), then an account you study, then fresh. Put 2 other options under the card as backup hooks, lettered A and B, in the same layout, each with its "Why it might work" line.
