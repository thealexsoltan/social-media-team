# Finding what worked, what flopped, and why

The numbers say **what** happened. This file is about the **why**, and how to say it honestly.

## The honesty rules

- Say "most likely", "best guess" or "early sign". Never "the reason", "proof" or "this works".
- Every claim comes with the counts or the sum behind it.
- A pattern in 1 post is a story, not a pattern. A pattern in 2 posts is an early sign. 3 or more, across different weeks, with nothing against it, can be strong.
- If something else changed (a holiday week, a boosted post, a new platform), say so next to the finding.
- **"A lot better" and "a lot worse" have fixed lines** (`outlier-score.md`): only for a score of 2.0 or more, or 0.5 or under (under 9 posts: about twice or about half the middle views). A 1.3 or a 1.5 is "a bit above normal", a 0.9 to 1.1 is "about normal": never "a lot better" or "a lot worse".
- **Name each rate exactly as it was worked out.** (likes + comments) ÷ views is "likes and comments per view", not "interactions ÷ views"; shares ÷ views is "shares per view". Never rename a rate to a bigger word.
- **"Brought the most" only on totals.** Say "X brought the most {link taps}" only if X's total really is the highest, and say it's the total. If it's only highest per 1,000 views, say exactly that.
- **Recount every claim about your own output** before it goes out: counts (posts, weeks, words, squares), "no day has two posts", how many squares are left and how many weeks they last, word counts of a source or a script (count them, never estimate), and any "every line", "every piece", "every idea", "none of them" claim. Check each against what you actually wrote. Not sure it's true? Drop the claim.
- **Recount before you write a count.** Every "x of y" is counted again from the rows just before it goes in the reply or a file.
- **"The only one" needs every value known.** If some first frames, hooks or tags are `not seen`, write "the only one we know of that didn't open on the dog ({N} first frames not known)", never "the only one".

Good: "Most likely the first frame. The two chain cleaning videos had the same topic and almost the same length. The one that opened on the black gunk dripping off the chain scored 2.4. The one that opened on you talking scored 0.4. That's one pair, so it's an early sign."

Bad: "Your audience loves before and afters. Talking head videos don't work."

## Rank by the goal

Views tell you about the hook. The goal number tells you about the business. Use the goal from `business-profile.md`:

| Goal | Rank by | Also show |
|---|---|---|
| Followers | follows per 1,000 views (follows ÷ views × 1,000) | raw follows |
| Leads, DMs, discovery calls | comments + shares, then link taps | per 1,000 views |
| Bookings or sales | link taps, then profile visits, then DMs, then follows | per 1,000 views |
| Getting seen | score | views |
| Other | ask the owner which number matters most | |

Per 1,000 views, rounded to one decimal: "12 link taps on 1,500 views is 8.0 per 1,000 views."

**Low views, high goal number:** point these out. "This one had fewer views than the rusty chain video, but more link taps. It may be the better one to copy if bookings are the goal."

**The goal number isn't in the data:** many bookings arrive by phone, text or walk-in, and no insights screen shows that. Say so plainly. Rank by the closest number you can see (profile visits, comments, link taps), and suggest a tally: ask every new customer "how did you hear about us?" and jot the answer down. Never turn views into an estimate of bookings.

## Look-alike pairs

The strongest lesson comes from two posts with **the same topic** and very different scores.

1. Find pairs: same topic (or very close), scores at least 2 times apart.
2. List everything you can see side by side: first line said, text on screen, first frame, format, length, day and time posted, caption.
3. Cross off what's the same. What's left is the best guess.
4. If more than one thing is different, say so: "Two things changed, the first frame and the length, so we can't tell which mattered. Next time, change only one."

Write it like this:

| | Winner | Flop |
|---|---|---|
| Post | 2026-10-06-chain-gunk-drip | 2026-10-03-what-is-a-tune-up |
| Score | 2.4 | 0.4 |
| First frame | black gunk dripping off a chain | owner talking |
| Text on screen | "this came off ONE chain" | "what is a tune-up?" |
| Length | 31 sec | 28 sec |
| Best guess | opening on the result, not on an explanation | |

No pair this week? Say "no look-alike pair this week" and move on. Don't force one.

## Why did this flop: the four hooks check

Use `four-hooks.md`. Go in this order and stop at the first "no":
1. **First frame:** could you tell the topic with the sound off? Words on screen in that first frame count: a card whose first frame has a text hook that names the topic passes this check.
2. **Text hook:** readable in under 2 seconds, and adds something to what's said?
3. **Spoken hook:** topic clear in the first sentence?
4. **First sound:** anything that grabs attention, or dead air and a slow "hey guys"?

For each hook, say what you checked it from: our card in `scripts/`, the owner's screenshot, the owner's words, or a guess from the caption. **A guess is only allowed when it's marked as one and rests on something really in the files** (a caption in `results-log.md` or `posts/`, a card): never "guessed from the captions" when there are no captions, and never a guess with nothing behind it. Nothing to go on: "can't check, I'd need {what}". If the card has a line for a hook, judge that line, and never call the hook missing unless the owner said they filmed it differently (a caption that matches a backup hook doesn't count: the Copywriter wrote it).

Under 9 posts there's no reliable normal, but the owner still gets a straight answer: "this one did a lot worse than your others (296 vs about 1,700). Most likely: {first no}." Mark it an early sign; never use that as a reason not to answer. If you can't see a hook at all, say "can't check, I'd need {what}". If every hook you can see looks fine, say so in a few words, then name the best candidate as your best guess: whatever sets this post apart from the others (the only offer or deadline post, posted at an odd time, a topic that only interests a few people, ran long). Good: "It was the only post with a deadline, which may be why." Bad: "I can't name a cause." Still "most likely", never sure.

## Counting patterns

For each tag (hook type, format, topic, length band, talks to "you" or "me"):
- how many posts had it
- how many of those were winners, and how many flops
- the middle score of those posts

Length bands: under 15 sec, 15 to 30, 31 to 45, 46 to 60, over 60.

**Why `from our card` counts.** A card the owner filmed as written tells us the real first frame, text, first line and ask, word for word. That's as good as the owner telling us. A card they didn't confirm is only a plan, so it counts as a guess.

## Which ask works

For bookings and leads, what the post asks people to do matters as much as the hook. Count it.

1. Use the same posts as the normal: each post's latest check, at least 3 days old, not boosted. Each platform on its own (different accounts). If the owner posts on more than one, show the main platform and add another only if it has 3 or more posts with a known ask.
2. Group the posts by their Call to action (`tags.md`). Leave out `not known`, and say how many you left out.
3. For each group: how many posts, how many winners ("2 of 4"), the goal number added up, the views added up, and the goal number per 1,000 views from those two totals. Do the sum (38 ÷ 6,200 × 1,000 = 6.1); write it in `## Working` in `results-log.md`. In the weekly report, give only the plain result ("Link in bio brought 38 link taps, the most"), with no per 1,000 figure unless the owner asked which ending works (then the one small table in `output-layouts.md`).
4. Rank the groups by the goal number per 1,000 views. Which number is the goal number: the first one in "Rank by the goal" above (follows for followers, comments plus shares for leads, link taps for bookings). Show profile visits next to it for bookings if you have them.
5. A group with 1 or 2 posts: give the count and write "too few to judge".

Say what each ask can and can't show:
- **Text**, **Call** and walk-ins never show up in insights. Write it plainly: "Texts and calls don't show up here, so we can't tell if this ask works without your tally."
- **Comment keyword** pushes comments up on purpose. For a leads goal that's the ask doing its job, but say the comments were asked for.
- **Link in bio** shows up as link taps and profile visits. It's the easiest ask to measure, so don't call it the winner just because it's the only one we can see.

Good, for `## Working` and the brief (a made-up bike shop): "Link in bio: 5 posts, 2 of 5 winners, 38 link taps on 6,200 views, 6.1 per 1,000. No ask: 9 posts, 2 of 9 winners, 41 on 12,400 views, 3.3 per 1,000. Text or call: 3 posts, 5 link taps on 2,900 views, but texts don't show up here. Most likely: asking for the link brings more taps than not asking. Early sign."

Bad: "Link in bio is your best call to action." (no counts, and ignores what we can't see)

## Same video, different platforms

When one short name has rows on 2 or more platforms in this check:

1. Score each row against **its own platform's** normal, as usual.
2. Put the scores side by side, one row per platform (layout in `output-layouts.md`).
3. Never set raw views side by side as a result. 3,000 views on one platform and 900 on another tell you nothing until each is compared with that platform's normal.
4. A platform with fewer than 9 posts old enough to count has no reliable normal. Write "no normal yet (only {N} {platform} posts, we need 9)" in its row, and say plainly that we can't tell yet how that platform treated the video.
5. One video is one data point. "Instagram pushed this one well above its normal" is fine. "Instagram is your better platform" is not, until several videos say the same.

## Your winning first lines

The owner's own openings that won are the best hooks the Scriptwriter can model. We save them as patterns in `performance-brief.md` under `## Your winning first lines`.

**Which posts can go in:**
- Winners only: a score of 2.0 or more, not boosted, from the post's latest check.
- Only with a reliable normal (9 or more posts on that platform).
- Only when the first line is **known**: "Where this came from" is `from our card`, `owner told us` or `screenshot`. A caption guess isn't what was said. For a text post, the first line of the post text in the export is the real first line, so it counts.
- No talking? Use the words on screen as the first line, and say so in the Real line ("(text on screen)").

If nothing passes, write "Not enough winners yet." and why: no reliable normal, no winners, or winners whose first line we don't know (then ask for those first lines).

**How to write the pattern:**
- Keep the sentence. Swap only the parts that change from post to post for `[blanks]`, at most 3.
- It still has to read as a sentence a person could say.
- If two or more winners share the same shape, they're one pattern: list the best one as the Real line and count them all in "Used in".
- Don't invent a pattern the lines don't show. "You don't have to [X] to [Y]" from "You don't have to cover your grey to hide it" is fine. Turning it into a list hook is not.

**Used in:** count every post with a known first line that used this shape, winners or not: "3 of 3 posts with this opening were winners". A pattern that also opened a flop says so: "2 of 3, the third scored 0.4".

**Confidence**, the same test as rules:
- **strong**: 3 or more winners used it, across at least 2 different weeks, the normal is reliable, and no post with this opening scored under 1.0.
- **early sign**: anything less (most patterns start here, from 1 or 2 winners).

Up to 5 entries, highest score first. Rewrite the whole section every week from the log, so a pattern that stops winning drops off by itself.

## Topics you over-use and under-use

For the monthly review only. Count from the Topic column of `results-log.md` across every scored post in the log (latest check each, not boosted). Say which dates that covers.

- **Posted a lot, rarely wins:** a topic with 4 or more posts where 1 or none were winners. "Schedule: 5 posts, 0 of 5 winners."
- **Posted rarely, mostly wins:** a topic with 1 to 3 posts where more than half were winners. "Nervous and older dogs: 2 posts, 2 of 2 winners." Under 3 posts, add "too few to judge" and suggest one TEST post on it.

These are leads for the plan, not rules. Over-used topics may still matter to the business (opening hours, offers). Say so, and suggest a new angle instead of dropping them.

A tag only becomes a rule when it clearly splits winners from the rest: "3 of 3 winners opened on a before and after; 0 of 3 flops did." Not: "before and afters averaged a bit higher".

## From pattern to rule

| What you have | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| 3 or more posts, at least 2 different weeks, nothing against it, normal is reliable, and at least 2 of those posts with known tags (`owner told us`, `screenshot` or `from our card`) | Rule, **strong** |
| 2 posts, or one look-alike pair, or anything while the normal isn't reliable, or tags mostly `guessed from caption` or `from our card, not confirmed` | Rule, **early sign** |
| 1 post, or an idea nobody has tried | **Try next** |
| A rule that 2 later posts broke | **Retired rules**, with the date and why |
| Something the owner told you | Rule or Try next, evidence "owner says"; never strong on that alone |

**"Do more of" lines come from a fresh count.** Count the posts in `results-log.md` that match the line's exact wording and give that count. Don't take a brief rule and narrow its wording (a rule about "opening on the dog" doesn't back "open on the dog's paws" unless the log shows it). Topics are counted from the log's Topic column only.

Each week, check last week's "Try next" items. If one was tested, say how it did and move it: up to a rule, or out.

Good rule lines:
- "Do show the finished bike in the first second, then go back to how it started. Evidence: 3 of 3 winners; 0 of 3 flops. Confidence: strong."
- "Don't post a price list as a video. Evidence: 2 of 2 price list videos scored under 0.5. Confidence: early sign."

Bad rule lines:
- "Post engaging content." (not something anyone can follow)
- "Reels get 3x more views." (a claim, not your data)
- "Always post at 6pm." (from one post)
