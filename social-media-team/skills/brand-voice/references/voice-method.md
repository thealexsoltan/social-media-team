# How to study someone's voice

Read every sample twice. The first time, just read. The second time, fill in this checklist. Write down a real example from the samples for each point you can.

## The checklist

**Who and to whom**
- Do they say "I" or "we"? Do they talk to "you"?
- Do they name the customer a special way? (pups, clients, homeowners, mamas)

**Length and rhythm**
- About how many words in a normal sentence? Count 5 sentences and take the middle one.
- Do they mix very short lines with longer ones? Do they use one line per sentence?
- Paragraphs: long blocks or lots of white space?

**Word level**
- Everyday words, or some trade words? Which trade words, and do they explain them?
- Contractions (don't, we're) or full words (do not, we are)?

**How they start**
- Question, bold statement, story, joke, a fact about the customer's problem?
- Copy the first line of each sample. Look for the pattern.

**How they end**
- A call to action? What exactly? (Book now, DM me, link in bio, call us, nothing)
- A sign-off? A catchphrase?

**Personality**
- Humour: none, dry, playful, puns?
- Energy: calm, warm, excited?
- Confidence: humble, matter-of-fact, bold?
- How they sell: soft (helpful tips, rarely asks), direct (asks clearly), story first?

**Habits**
- Emoji: which ones, how many per post, where (start, end, every line)?
- Punctuation: exclamation marks, ALL CAPS words, no full stops, lots of questions?
- Phrases they repeat across samples. These are gold. List them word for word.

**Spoken voice (from voice notes or video transcripts)**
- Filler words they actually use (so, honestly, look, right)?
- How they'd open a video if a friend asked a question?
- Do they explain with examples, stories or steps?

## Learning from winners (Mixed and path B)

Three layers, in this order. Work only from words you can actually see: a caption, a transcript, a screenshot. A link alone shows nothing.

### Layer 1: break down each winning post

Take one post at a time and answer this, in full, before you write any headings:

> Break down the structure of this post so that I can recreate it from scratch. Break down why it works, the psychological patterns involved, what context is needed from me, and anything else I would need to understand how to recreate it.

Then write the breakdown under the same headings every time:
1. **The post:** account, platform, views, its score ("4,200 ÷ normal 900 = 4.7"), where the words came from (`Apify`, `screenshot`, `owner pasted`, `transcript`).
2. **How it's built:** the opener, the middle and the ending, in a few lines each.
3. **Why it works:** what makes someone stop, keep watching and act. Mark it "best guess".
4. **The psychology:** the feeling it uses (curiosity, "that's me", relief, surprise, a little fear of missing out) and the patterns behind it (an open loop, a number that sounds too big, naming the viewer's exact situation, saying what others won't).
5. **What you'd need to write one:** the facts, story, belief or result from the owner this shape needs.
6. **Anything else to recreate it:** length, pacing, where the words sit on screen, what the first frame shows, what it leaves out.
7. **The shape:** the post's moves, described in your own words ("opens on a cost nobody mentions, then one fix"), with `[blanks]` where the owner's facts go. Never keep the creator's own wording around the blanks.

Never copy their sentences, stories, names, numbers or claims. Only the shape.

**Who a shape points at.** If a shape blames someone ("Your [person] told you..."), write who it may and may not point at for this owner. Never at the owner's own trade, the people they work with or refer to, or their customers.

### Layer 2: combine them into one comprehensive guide

Combine all of the breakdowns into a single comprehensive guide: `style-guide.md`, layout in `files-layout.md`. It's the full playbook the writers work from, so make it complete, not a summary.

- Put the breakdowns side by side. Count what repeats ("5 of 8 posts open with a mistake the viewer is making").
- Group the posts into **post types** (2 to 5): each one gets how it's built step by step, why it works, the psychology, what it needs from the owner, the shape, and which posts it came from.
- A pattern needs 2 or more posts. A pattern seen in one post goes under "Seen once only".
- Then put a short version (5 to 7 lines) in "How our posts are built" in the voice guide, ending "Full guide: `style-guide.md`". The written and spoken voice sections come from the owner's own words (Mixed), or from their answers in Layer 3 (path B).
- Accounts disagree? Say so and pick the shape that fits the owner's business and goal.
- Check every shape against the owner's "Words we never use" and banned topics. A shape that needs a banned word gets changed or dropped.
- Test lines built from a shape use the owner's words **and** sentence habits (how they open, whether they ask questions). A shape is a route, not a costume.

### Layer 3: ask only what's missing

From the combined guide, work out what you still need from the owner to write in that style, and ask it in **one message, 5 questions at most**. Usually:
1. Who do you help, and with what? Say it like you'd tell a friend.
2. What's one thing most people in your niche get wrong, that you'd happily argue about?
3. Two or three short true stories or results from your work. (Never invented. "Not yet" is a fine answer.)
4. How do you talk to the people watching: "friendly neighbour", "calm expert", "straight talker", or something else? (Winners often sound blunt. You don't have to.) And do you use emoji, swear or joke around?
5. How do you talk out loud? A 60-second voice note is best. Your phone can turn it into text.

Never-say words come from the profile's question 6, so don't ask them again. Skip any question the profile or the chat already answers in full. The owner can skip them all: then mark the guide "built from winners only, it'll sound more like you once you answer the 5 questions".

### Layer 3b: going deeper (optional)

Under the 5 questions, offer one line: "Want posts with more of you in them? Answer 6 more questions about what you stand for. Skip this and we can do it later." If they say yes (or answer them), ask in one message:
1. Why should people listen to you? Who are you, in a sentence or two?
2. What does your best customer worry about at night, and what do they secretly want?
3. What do you believe that most people in your niche don't?
4. What's the before and after? Where are people when they find you, and where are they after?
5. Your story in a few lines: where you started, the turning point, where you are now.
6. How do you want people to feel after a post? Any topics or feelings you won't go near?

Put the answers in "What we stand for" in the voice guide, in their words, never invented. Writers use these for topics, stories and opinions.

### Layer 4: prove it with 9 first drafts

Once the owner approves the voice guide, write **3 drafts of each of the top 3 post types** from `style-guide.md` (9 in all). Pick the 3 types (and the topics inside them) where the owner's facts are most complete; a draft that would be mostly `[blanks]` is swapped for one that isn't, and the missing fact goes to the open questions instead. Use only the owner's facts, stories and answers, in their voice, with `[blanks]` for anything they haven't given. For video platforms write each one as the spoken first lines plus the on-screen text; for text platforms, the whole post. Head them "First drafts to try. The Scriptwriter and Copywriter finish the ones you like." Run the shapes check and the Build self-check on them first. In Cowork or Claude Code save them to `first-drafts.md`; in chat show them and give the file block. In Cowork or Claude Code, show only each draft's first line in chat and point to the file. Then checkpoint: "Which ones do you like?". When they pick, mark those drafts `(picked YYYY-MM-DD)` in `first-drafts.md`, and give one next step: the Scriptwriter turns picked video drafts into filming cards (/scriptwriter), the Copywriter finishes picked text posts (/copywriter). If fewer than 2 other accounts were studied, add one line that the Content Researcher can find more winners later.

## Red flags (AI-sounding writing to remove in Check mode)

- Long dashes or short dashes used as punctuation
- (Never flag a phrase the owner uses in their own samples, even one from this list.)
- "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "game-changer", "unlock", "elevate", "journey", "seamless", "look no further"
- Three-item lists everywhere ("fast, friendly, and reliable")
- "It's not just X, it's Y"
- Questions nobody asked ("Ever wondered why...?") if the owner never writes that way
- Perfect, formal grammar when the owner writes casually
- Sentences that say nothing ("Quality matters.")
- Hashtag walls and emoji the owner never uses

## Good "sounds like / doesn't" pairs

Make the two lines say the same thing, so the only difference is the voice.

| Sounds like us | Doesn't |
|---|---|
| "Sourdough's out at 7. Last week it was gone by 9." | "Indulge in our artisanal freshly baked sourdough, crafted with passion every morning." |

## When the samples disagree

Owners write differently on different platforms. If Instagram captions are playful and emails are formal, say so in the guide and note which one to use where. If one sample looks very different from the rest, ask the owner if they wrote it.
