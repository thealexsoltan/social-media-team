# How the Content Strategist builds ideas and plans

Read `idea-mining.md` and `content-matrix.md` first. This file adds where the matrix's ideas come from, how to pick posts from the matrix, how to place them in the week, and the topic check for growing one idea (section 8).

## 1. Fill the idea bank: mine first, then fill the matrix

The idea bank is built around the content matrix (`content-matrix.md`): 5 to 7 topics × 8 post styles, 40 to 56 squares. Mine the sources below first, in this order. Each mined idea goes into the square it fits (`content-matrix.md`, section 4) and keeps its Source and Tag, so the owner can see why it's there. Squares nothing fits get a fresh headline from the owner's facts (Source `Your topics`, Tag TEST).

| Order | Source | How many | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your winners ("Best posts to copy from" in `performance-brief.md`, scores of 2.0 or more in `results-log.md`) | 2 to 3 new versions of each winner | Use "swap one thing" from `idea-mining.md`. Tag REPEAT. Each "Try next" item in the brief becomes a TEST idea |
| 2 | Their winners ("Top hooks" and "Patterns to borrow" in `research-notes.md`) | 1 to 2 versions of each winner | Keep what made it work, make it about the owner's customers and area. Tag REMIX |
| 3 | Customer questions | 1 idea per question, more if it's rich | Usually TEST, unless a winner already covers the same question |
| 4 | What's new ("What's new in your niche" in `research-notes.md`, a quick web search, or the calendar) | 3 to 6 | Seasons, holidays, local events, news in the niche. Respect any "Use by" date. Tag TEST. Optional: if you can search, run the topic check (section 8) on 1 or 2 of the owner's main topics; each finding becomes a TEST idea with a `Topic check` Source (`strategist-files.md`) |
| 5 | Your topics (the matrix rows) | Every empty square gets a fresh headline | Tag TEST |
| 6 | Swaps | Extra ideas on top of the grid, as many as the winners deserve | Swap one thing on the best ideas above. Same Topic and Style as the square they came from |

The matrix spreads ideas across every topic and style by itself: every row has 8. Mined ideas only decide *which* headline sits in a square, never leave a square empty or put two in the grid.

Before you add an idea, check the "Already covered" list and the existing ideas. Don't add the same idea twice in different words.

When refreshing an existing bank, add new ideas with the next free IDs. Never renumber or delete old ones.

### When sources are missing

| What's missing | What to do | Say this to the owner (or close) |
|---|---|---|
| No results yet (new account or never measured) | No REPEAT ideas. Lean on REMIX and customer questions | "You don't have results yet, so no idea is a REPEAT. I've leaned on what's working for others and on your customers' questions." |
| No `research-notes.md` | No REMIX ideas, unless the owner pasted or described posts they like in this chat. Customer questions, what's new and your topics carry the bank. Most ideas are TEST | "I haven't got any research on who's winning in your niche, so most of these are tests. The Content Researcher can fix that. Type /content-researcher." |
| Research notes older than 7 days | Use them, and suggest a refresh | "Your research is {N} days old. I've used it, but a quick refresh from the Content Researcher would give us fresher winners." |
| No customer questions | Ask for 5, unless the owner already gave them, said they have none, or the Orchestrator asked (see SKILL.md for when to ask first and when to ask just above the checkpoint) | "What do customers ask you every week? Those are often your best ideas." |
| No web search | Use the calendar and what the owner told you. Never present a guess as news | |

Never invent a competitor, a post, a view count, a score, a trend or a statistic to fill a gap. An honest TEST beats a fake REMIX.

## 2. Pick posts for the plan (from the matrix)

Posts are picked from matrix squares, and every brief names its square (`strategist-files.md`). At least 3 posts in 4 come from squares; the rest are offer posts or timely posts that fit no square. Pick about 1 idea in 3. The rest stay in the bank for later weeks, so say how long the matrix lasts at this pace.

Pick in this order:
1. **The 5 starting picks** (the matrix's "Start with these 5") open the plan, in weeks 1 and 2, as far as filming time allows.
2. **Proof first:** squares built on REPEAT ideas (the owner's winners), then REMIX squares with the highest scores.
3. **Customer questions** that match the owner's main goal (bookings, sales, leads).
4. **Timely ideas** for the dates they fall on (a holiday post goes in that week, not after it).
5. **Easy to film:** when two ideas are equal, pick the one that's quicker to make.

**Weight toward what's working, where we have proof.** Give more posts to the rows (topics), columns (styles) and formats that winners point to, and say so in one line of the summary ("More myth-busters and nail posts this month, because that's what's winning for accounts like yours."):
- The owner's own results first: the topics, styles and formats of their winners, and the "Rules to follow" in `performance-brief.md` (strong rules beat everything here).
- No results yet? The "Formats that win", "Hook types that win" and "Topics that win" tables in `research-notes.md`, or `study/playbook.md` if there is one.
- No proof at all? Spread evenly and follow the matrix's weekly rhythm.
- **Claim only the weighting the plan shows.** Before you write that line (or a "Where the weight went" note), count the plan's posts per topic, style and format. Say only what the counts show ("4 of 12 posts are myth-busters"), and name where the weighting came from ("your winners in `performance-brief.md`", "the Formats that win table in `research-notes.md`"). Even counts: say it's spread evenly.
- **A flop is never a fresh TEST.** An owner post that scored under 0.5 is never offered again as a new TEST. Either REMIX it with the one change named ("same topic, opens on the result this time, because the first one opened on you talking") with the old post as its Source, or leave it out.

**Spread across the matrix,** unless the owner's results say otherwise:
- A 4-week plan uses at least 5 of the 8 styles and every topic at least once.
- No style more than twice in one week.
- Now vs next posts are guesses about the future: at most one a week.

Then check each pick against these questions. Drop or swap any that fail two or more:
- Will a lot of the owner's customers care?
- Can the owner show it on camera, or with a photo, easily?
- Is there an obvious strong opening (a mistake, a surprise, a before and after)?
- Is it the right time for it?
- Does it say something the owner's niche isn't already saying in the same way?

### The mix

- A healthy week is about half REPEAT or REMIX and half TEST.
- New account with no results: lean on REMIX and customer questions. Still keep at least one TEST a week so we learn something.
- No research and no results: mostly TEST is fine and honest. Say so in the plan's "Built from" line.
- Never more than 2 posts in a row on the same topic.
- Never the same idea twice in one week. Two angles of one idea are fine if they're on different days.

## 3. Size the plan to real filming time

- **Videos per week = the number the owner can film.** Take it from what they told you this week, else from `business-profile.md` ("Videos per week we can film"). Never plan more videos than that.
- Profile says "not sure"? Plan 2 videos a week and say so: "I've planned 2 videos a week. Tell me if you can do more or less."
- **No-filming posts** (Carousel, Photo, Text post, Story, see Format values in `strategist-files.md`) can sit on top: up to 2 a week, or more only if the owner asks. They need no camera time. Fit them to the platforms: Facebook and LinkedIn suit text and photo posts; Instagram suits carousels and stories.
- **The matrix's weekly rhythm** sets the days and jobs (teach, build trust, ask for bookings) when the plan doesn't have better days from results.
- **One shoot, several videos:** if the week has 2 or more videos, suggest filming them together and write it in the week's "Filming plan" line. Use the owner's filming day from this chat or the profile's "Filming day" line. "Whenever I'm on a job" or "any day" is a real answer: write "on your next job where {what the videos need}" and no weekday. Only if nothing says when, pick a day, write "(my guess)" after it, and log the question (SKILL.md, Facts the owner hasn't given). Every day or work schedule named in the line ("during Monday's grooms", "put it together by Sunday") either comes from the owner or gets "(my guess)" too, and a guessed day stays the same in every week of the plan. Never put a "(my guess)" day over an answer the owner gave.
- **Not on camera?** No Talking head: use Hands at work, Voice over clips, Text on screen only or Slideshow. "Sometimes" on camera? Mix Talking head with those.

## 4. Place posts in the week

- Spread posts across the week. Don't stack 3 on one day.
- **Every named day you picked gets "(my guess)".** A weekday or date that didn't come from the owner (this chat or the profile's "Filming day") is followed by "(my guess)", in plan files (Filming plan lines, briefs, notes) and in replies alike: "film on Tuesday (my guess)". Posting dates in the plan table are the plan itself and need no mark.
- **Season check.** Before a timely idea goes in, check it against the date and place: no summer topics in November, no snow in a place that doesn't get it, unless there's a stated reason (a trip, a far away customer base). Say the reason in its brief.
- If `performance-brief.md` names good days or times, use them. If not, spread them out and say: "I've spread these out. Once we have results, the Data Analyst can tell us your best days."
- Put booking or sales posts a day or two **before** the days the owner wants to fill (for example, if Tuesdays are quiet, ask for bookings on Sunday or Monday).
- Timely posts go before the date, never after (a winter-prep post before the first freeze, a holiday post before the holiday).
- Cross-posting: one video can go to the main platform and the others on the same day. One row.

## 5. Pick the CTA route

Use `CTA values` in `strategist-files.md`. Then:
- Every week has at least one post that asks directly for the owner's main goal (book, buy, call).
- How-to and myth posts usually get a soft ask (save, follow). Stories and offer posts get the direct ask.
- **Offer posts** use the one team definition in `files-layout.md`: a helpful post that just ends with the booking line is not one. Plan about one offer post in four, never 2 in any 4 in a row. When you tell the owner how many posts sell, count only offer posts and name them, so you and the Copywriter give the same answer.
- Comment-a-keyword only if the profile says there's a comment-to-DM tool, and only on the platforms it works on. If not, use "link in bio", a phone number, or "send me a message", whichever the profile names.

## 6. Write the brief

Keep each brief to the fields in `strategist-files.md`, one line each. The brief is direction, not the script:
- **Angle:** the way in, in one line.
- **Hook direction:** what the first 2 seconds must do ("open on the cracked cookie", "show the before photo first"). Don't write the final hook or the script. That's the Scriptwriter.
- **Why it should work:** name the source and the reason. Good: "REMIX: @crumb.and.crate.cookies in research-notes got a score of 2.6 by showing one order from start to finish. Same idea, your birthday cookie boxes." Bad: "This format goes viral." If there's no proof, say what we're testing: "TEST: your customers ask this every week. We'll see if it pulls bookings."

## 7. The weekly update (Next week mode)

1. Find the coming week: the next Monday to Sunday after today, unless the owner names a week. If today is Monday, the week starts today (the shared rule in `files-layout.md`).
2. Read the newest `performance-brief.md` and `results-log.md`. Pull out: winners (score 2.0 or more, and "Best posts to copy from"), flops, the "Rules to follow" (each starts with Do or Don't) and "Try next". Rules marked strong beat the mix guide above. Rules marked early sign are worth a test, not the whole week. "Try next" items become TEST posts. A TEST idea that a new winner now proves may be re-tagged REPEAT: change its Source to that winner post and its score, copied from the file.
3. If that week is already in the plan:
   - Keep posts with status scripted, filming, ready or posted exactly as they are.
   - Posts still at `idea` may be swapped for something the results now favour. List every swap and why.
4. If that week isn't in the plan, add it at the bottom with the next P numbers.
5. Look at last week. Posts the owner already said are posted: mark them `posted`, update the `Status:` line in their post files and cards, and don't ask about them. Any other post not marked `posted`? List them and ask if they should move into this week. Don't move them on your own.
6. Check the later weeks. Read every brief against the brief's "Rules to follow". If a post still at `idea` now clashes with a rule, fix its brief (hook direction, format or angle). If a clashing post is past `idea`, flag it, don't change it. Sum it up in one line in the reply, naming posts by title and date: "Also changed: the leak video (2026-11-24) and the slab video (2026-12-01) now open on the problem, not on you talking (your rule from results)."
7. Re-check every post you moved or swapped: offer spacing, filming per week, and posting days (SKILL.md, Change the plan).
8. Update the idea bank: mark new picks `planned P{n}`, mark posted ones `used P{n}`, add posted titles to "Already covered", and add new ideas from fresh winners. A fresh winner's square gets 2 more ideas (REPEAT, naming the winner and its score), and its row and column get more of next week's posts. A style or topic that flopped twice in a row gets fewer posts, never removed from the grid. New picks come from unused squares first.
9. Update the plan's header: Last updated, Covers, Mix so far.

## 8. The topic check (Grow one idea)

A quick look at what people get wrong about a topic and what they argue about, done before the 5 angles. It gives the mistake, myth and surprise angles real material instead of blanks. It's direction for the owner to confirm, never a fact on its own.

**The owner comes first.** Before searching, look in the chat and the owner's files (customer questions, their samples, `learned-rules.md`, `research-notes.md`). Anything the owner already said about the topic beats anything on the web. Use it as theirs, with no link needed. If a finding disagrees with the owner, keep the owner's take and show the finding as one to check, never as a correction.

**Can't search the web?** Say so in one line ("I can't search the web here, so the myth and surprise angles have blanks for you."). Skip the check. Keep the blanks in the angles (`[the myth you hear most about this]`) and log one question for them (SKILL.md, Facts the owner hasn't given). Never fill the gap from memory and call it research.

### How to search

- 2 or 3 searches, no more. This is a quick check, not a research project.
- **What people get wrong:** the topic plus words like myth, mistake, wrong, "do I need to". Prefer pages from people who know the trade: trade bodies, makers' care or safety guides, councils and government sites, vets or health bodies for pets and bodies.
- **What people argue about:** the topic plus "vs", debate, should I, or a forum name. A forum thread proves people argue about it. It doesn't prove who's right, so write it as "some say X, others say Y". `People argue about` needs both sides on the page (or two sources); one business's blog is `People get it wrong` at most. Each finding says only what the page says: no added symptoms, no dropped words that tilt it.
- **Local business?** One of the searches may add the town or area (local rules, local weather, local water), if it fits the topic.
- **Trade debates only.** No politics, no religion, nothing that picks on a group of people.
- **Open the page.** Only use what you read on the page itself, never just a search snippet. Can't open it? Don't use it.
- Never invent a link, a page, a number or what a page says.

### What to keep

- 2 to 4 findings, each one line in plain words, written in your own words (no long quotes from the page).
- Each finding gets an ID (`F1`, `F2`), a kind (`People get it wrong` or `People argue about`), its link, and the check mark `check this is true for you`.
- Numbers only if the page states them, and they keep the link. If a number matters but the owner's own experience could differ, leave it out.
- No other businesses, brands or products named in the finding line. The link already says where it came from.
- Nothing useful found? Say so in one line and treat it like no search (blanks plus a question). Don't stretch a weak page into a finding.

The layout is in `strategist-files.md` ("Grown ideas").

### Using findings in the angles

- The myth, the mistake and the surprise angles may use a finding. Name it in the angle line, for example "Store drain cleaner sits on top of the clog (F1)".
- The story angle never comes from the web. It's the owner's real story or a blank.
- The how-to angle comes from the owner's own way of doing it. A finding can point at the topic, but the steps are theirs: leave a blank for any step they haven't told you.

### Until the owner confirms

- Log one question per grown idea, not one per finding: "Under I15 I found F1 and F2. Are they true for how you work?" (SKILL.md, Facts the owner hasn't given).
- When the owner answers, change the mark: `you confirmed`, or `you said no`. A finding marked `you said no` is never used. An angle that leaned on it goes back to a blank, or turns into the owner's own take if they gave one.
- If an angle with an unconfirmed finding goes into the plan, its brief keeps the mark in the Angle line: "(F1 on I15, not confirmed yet)", so the Scriptwriter knows to check before it's said on camera.
