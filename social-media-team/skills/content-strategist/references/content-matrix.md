# The content matrix

The content matrix is how the Strategist finds ideas. The owner's topics are the rows, 8 post styles are the columns, and every square holds one specific headline idea. 5 topics give 40 ideas, 6 give 48, 7 give 56. Then we fill the squares from what's already working (the owner's winners, other accounts' winners, customer questions, what's new) before we write anything fresh.

This is our own method. Never name another content method, its maker or where it came from, in a reply or a file.

## 1. The three things you need

| You need | Where to find it first | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **The business** | `business-profile.md` ("What we sell", "Best customer", "Where"), `voice-guide.md` ("What we stand for") | What they do, who they serve, what makes them different |
| **The outcome** | "Best customer" (what they want), "Owner's answers" | The change customers get, in plain words ("a clean, happy dog without the drive and the wait") |
| **5 to 7 topics** | "Topics we post about" | The subjects the owner wants to be known for. These become the rows |

Use the files. Ask only for what's still missing, **all in one message**, never one question at a time. Put a short example under each question. If you ask for topics too, offer 5 to 7 that fit their kind of business.

- **Only the topics are missing?** Don't ask. Propose them and build (the "None" rule below).
- **The outcome isn't written anywhere?** Read it from "Best customer" if it's clear there. Ask only if you'd be guessing.
- **No profile at all?** SKILL.md, Step 0, item 1 (send to Brand Voice, or the 6 quick questions). Don't make up your own intake.
- Once you have the three things, **build straight away.** No follow-up questions before the matrix.

### Topic rules

| What the owner has | What you do |
|---|---|
| 5 to 7 topics | Use them as the rows. Only tidy the wording |
| 1 to 4 topics | Keep theirs and add your own to reach 5 to 7. Mark yours "(suggested)" |
| 8 or more | Group the ones that overlap into 5 to 7. The extras become angles inside a row. Show the grouping |
| None, or "I don't know" | Propose 5 to 7 from the business, the outcome and any research, each with a one-line "what it helps with": what it does for the customer ("helps you keep your dog calm at the vet"), not what the posts show ("videos of calm dogs"). Build the matrix in the same reply. Don't wait for approval |

Keep proper nouns capitalised as the owner writes them (town names, brands, breeds that are names, holidays): "Asheville", "Labrador", "Thanksgiving".

Never quietly drop a topic the owner gave you. Topics marked "(suggested)" in the profile are fine to use as they are.

Suggested or grouped topics live in the idea bank's matrix section. Don't change `business-profile.md` unless the owner says yes to them.

## 2. The 8 post styles (always this order)

The columns never change and never move. Use these plain labels everywhere: in the reply, the idea bank's Style column and square names.

| # | Style | Job (what the reader gets) | Headline shapes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **How-to** | Practical steps to do one specific thing | "How to [result] (without [obstacle])" · "The [n]-step [thing]" |
| 2 | **Story** | A real story that shows what's possible | "The story behind [real moment]" · "How [real person or dog] went from [start] to [result]" · "Share a real time when..." |
| 3 | **Why it works** | Explains why something happens, works or fails | "Why [surprising thing] happens" · "What actually happens during [process]" |
| 4 | **Myth-buster** | Pushes back on common advice, gives a better view | "Stop [common habit]. [Do this] instead." · "[Belief] isn't [what people think]" |
| 5 | **Spotted** | A pattern or habit most people miss | "The hidden [cost or problem] nobody talks about" · "What [behaviour] says about [thing]" |
| 6 | **This vs that** | Compares two options so the reader can choose | "[A] vs. [B]: which is better for [goal]?" |
| 7 | **Now vs next** | How people do it today vs how they may do it soon | "From [today] to [next]: how [thing] could change" · "Why [thing] may soon [shift]" |
| 8 | **List** | A numbered list of tips, mistakes, signs or questions | "[5 to 9] [mistakes, ways, signs or questions] for [goal]" |

A square is named `{Topic} × {Style}`, for example "Nail trims × Myth-buster".

**Style is not a hook type or a format.** Style is what the post does for the reader (the column). Hook type is how its first 2 seconds open, and format is what it looks like: both come only from `formats.md`. A How-to square can be filmed as Hands at work and open with a Warning.

## 3. Rules for every square

- **One idea per square.** The 8 ideas in a row are 8 different angles on the topic, not one idea reworded.
- **A full headline sentence of about 8 to 18 words.** Never a keyword.
- **Only the owner's facts.** Their place, services, customer types, phrases, real stories and numbers from their files or this chat. No price, time, count or result they didn't give you. The number at the start of a List headline is how many points the post will have, not a claim, so it's fine.
- **Every idea moves the reader toward the outcome.**
- **Every idea really fits its column:**
  - A myth-buster names the exact belief it breaks.
  - A list starts with a number from 5 to 9.
  - This vs that names two real options, written either "A vs B" or "A or B" ("Shave or brush out?"). Both are fine.
  - Now vs next compares how people do it today with how they may do it soon, worded with "could" or "may", never as a fact.
- **Story squares need a real story.** Use one from the owner's samples or files (by name, as they told it), else write it as a prompt with a blank, not a finished headline: "The story behind [the day you...]", "Share a real time when [a customer...]". Never make up a customer, a dog, a name or what happened.
- **The owner's rules apply:** `learned-rules.md` (what customers are called, banned words) and the profile's "Never say or show". Never write a banned word, even to say it's banned.
- No made-up statistics, results, reviews or guarantees. No empty clickbait.

## 4. Fill the squares from what's working

Before you write a fresh headline, look for proof. Work through the sources in the order in `plan-method.md`, section 1, and put each mined idea into the square it fits best:
- **Row:** the idea's topic.
- **Column:** the style its shape matches.

| The mined idea looks like... | It usually fits |
|---|---|
| a warning ("please don't [common thing]") | Myth-buster |
| a before and after, or the hidden problem shown close up | Why it works or Spotted |
| a scared or stuck customer and the payoff | Story |
| "do this [small thing]", steps, a demo | How-to |
| "3 signs...", "5 things..." | List |
| a customer question about choosing ("should I...", "is X or Y better") | This vs that |
| a customer question about how or how often | How-to |
| a season, a holiday, a change in the niche | Now vs next, or the style its shape fits |

Then:
- The square's headline is written from that idea, and the idea keeps its **Source** and **Tag** (`strategist-files.md`). A square built on a winner names that winner and its score, copied from the file.
- **Two mined ideas want the same square?** The one with more proof (REPEAT before REMIX, then the higher score) takes it. The other goes into another style in the same row if it honestly fits, or becomes an extra idea with the same Topic and Style (a new ID; the grid still shows the first).
- **A square with no proof** gets a fresh headline from the owner's facts. Source `Your topics`, Tag TEST.
- **Ideas that fit no square** (an offer post, a holiday booking reminder) still go in the bank, with Style `none (offer)` or `none (timely)`.

Extras that don't make the grid are still worth keeping: new versions of winners, swaps, a topic check finding. Say how many in one line of the reply.

Then, in this order: **Questions for you** (if any, at most 2, SKILL.md "Facts the owner hasn't given"), the saved files (or file blocks and the keep-it line), the **What next** line (run on your own only), and the checkpoint last.

**What next** line (one line, above the checkpoint): "Want more? Name a square (like "Nail trims × List") and I'll grow it, ask for 3 ideas per square, or pick one topic to go deeper."

The checkpoint's option 1 is "Looks good, make my 30-day plan from it".

### How to fill the extras

- **What you're really selling:** one sentence, the "not just X, you're selling Y" shape. Y uses the owner's or the profile's words for what customers want.
- **Start with these 5:** headlines word for word from the matrix. Pick in this order: squares built on the owner's winners, then other accounts' winners (highest score first), then customer questions that serve the main goal. Then balance them: at least one that brings new people (How-to, List or Myth-buster), one that builds trust (Story or Why it works), and one that leads toward the main goal. No more than 2 from one topic, at least 3 different styles. Their formats must fit what the owner can film (`plan-method.md`, section 3). These 5 open the 30-day plan.
- **Weekly rhythm:** one row per post a week: the videos they can film plus the no-filming posts (`plan-method.md`, section 3). Use the best days from `performance-brief.md` if it names them. Otherwise spread the days and put the "ask for bookings" day a day or two before the days the owner wants to fill. The weeks line is the number of ideas divided by posts a week, rounded down.
- **Mix:** three parts that add up to 100. The "ask for" part matches offer posts (`files-layout.md`): about one post in four or fewer, so 25% at most.
- **Core message:** one short line built from the owner's own words where you can ("no crate, no car" beats anything you'd invent).

## 6. Growing the matrix (Grow one idea mode)

The owner can grow it three ways. Same square rules as section 3 every time.

| The owner asks for... | You make | Saved as |
|---|---|---|
| one idea or one square ("grow Nail trims × List") | 5 angles: mistake, surprise, how-to, story, myth (SKILL.md, Grow one idea) | "Grown ideas", under that square's idea ID |
| 3 ideas per square (for one row, one column or the whole matrix) | 2 more ideas in each square, so 3 sit together | New IDs in the Ideas table, same Topic and Style |
| one topic, deeper | 5 to 7 subtopics of that topic as rows × the same 8 styles | "Deeper matrix: {topic}" section, new IDs, Topic written `{topic}: {subtopic}` |

**3 per square:** the three ideas in a square must differ in how they work, not just in wording: each solves a different problem, uses a different lever, or fits a different situation. Show them in the same two tables, the three ideas in one cell as "1) ... 2) ... 3) ...". The whole matrix gives 80 to 112 new ideas, so for the whole thing check once in one line that the owner wants it all ("That's 96 more ideas. All of them, or one row first?"), unless they already said all.

**Deeper topic:** reply with the title (`# {Topic}: a deeper matrix`), the count line, the subtopics with a one-line purpose each, the two tables and the caution line. No new rhythm or mix: those belong to the main matrix.

## 7. When the matrix changes

- **Topics change** (the owner adds, drops or renames one): rebuild the grid. Old ideas stay in the Ideas table with their IDs. Note the date in the matrix section: "Rebuilt YYYY-MM-DD: {what changed}".
- **New winners come in** (Next week mode): a winning square gets 2 more ideas (REPEAT, naming the winner and its score), and its row and column get more posts in the plan (`plan-method.md`, section 2).
- Never renumber, delete or quietly reword an idea that's planned or used.
