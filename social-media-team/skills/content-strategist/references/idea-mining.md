# Mining ideas instead of inventing them

Good ideas are found, not dreamed up. We look in three places, then grow each idea.

## The three sources

| Source | Where | What to take |
|---|---|---|
| **Your winners** | `results-log.md`, `performance-brief.md` | Posts with an outlier score of 2.0 or more. Make another version |
| **Their winners** | `research-notes.md` | Competitor posts with a score of 2.0 or more. Make your own version for your customers |
| **What's new** | `research-notes.md` ("What's new in your niche"), or a quick web search | News, seasons, local events, questions customers are asking right now |

Plus the **owner's own head**: the questions customers ask every week. These are often the best ideas of all. Ask for 5.

## Where mined ideas go: the content matrix

The Content Strategist puts every mined idea into a square of the content matrix: its topic is the row, and the post style its shape matches is the column (How-to, Story, Why it works, Myth-buster, Spotted, This vs that, Now vs next, List, always in that order). A square built on a winner keeps that winner's name and score. Squares nothing fits get a fresh idea from the owner's facts, tagged TEST. Details: the Strategist's `content-matrix.md`.

## Swap one thing

To turn a winner into a new idea, keep what made it work and change **one** thing:
- same topic, new format (talking video to a before and after)
- same format, new topic
- same hook style, new customer problem
- same idea, new customer type (families to older owners)

Change too much and you lose what made it work.

## Grow it: topic > idea > 5 angles

- **Topic**: a broad subject from `business-profile.md` (for example "nail trims").
- **Idea**: one specific point ("long nails change how a dog walks").
- **Angle**: one way into that idea. Aim for 5:
  1. the mistake ("you're probably trimming too little")
  2. the surprise ("why your dog's nails click on the floor")
  3. the how-to ("trim without the quick bleeding")
  4. the story ("Pepper couldn't walk the stairs")
  5. the myth ("walks don't wear nails down enough")

Every angle can be its own video. One idea can fill a week.

The mistake, surprise and myth angles can lean on a quick topic check: the owner's own take first, then what people get wrong or argue about online, each finding with its link and marked "check this is true for you" until the owner confirms. An angle that uses a finding names it, like (F1). A brief that says "F1 on I15, not confirmed yet" means nobody should say that point on camera or in a caption as a fact until the owner confirms it. Never a found claim without its link, never a guess.

## Tags

Tag every idea in the plan so we can learn later:
- **REPEAT**: a new version of a proven winner (yours)
- **REMIX**: a winner from someone else, made your own
- **TEST**: something new we haven't proved yet

A healthy week is about half REPEAT or REMIX and the rest TEST. A new account with no winners yet leans on REMIX.
